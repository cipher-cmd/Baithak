# India filters

The Chair runs these in Step 2 and writes the results into `india-checks.md`. Seats read that file in every round. Numbers marked `(rule of thumb)` are heuristics from operator experience, not sourced statistics. Say so when you use them.

## 1. Who pays: the four tiers

Every idea declares one primary tier. Mixing tiers is a failure mode on its own. Examples: an India 1 convenience product funded by India 3 ad revenue, or expecting $50/month SaaS revenue from India 2 traders.

| Tier | Who | Behaviour | What they pay for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **India 1** | Affluent, English-first consumers; top enterprises and funded startups | Trades money to save time. Buys convenience, status, speed | Subscriptions, premium D2C, quick commerce, high-ACV B2B. The market is shallow: never accept a "100M users" plan here |
| **India 2** | Aspirational middle class; GST-registered MSMEs, traders, clinics, agencies. UPI-native, WhatsApp-first | Trades time to save money. Uses free workarounds until forced | New revenue, tax or compliance, stopping leakage or theft, credit access, software bundled with hardware |
| **India 3** | Mass Bharat: micro-merchants, informal sector. Vernacular, voice and video first, low-end phones, cash and COD | No budget for software subscriptions; low trust in new brands | Ads, micro-transactions, embedded credit, take rates through aggregators, community distribution |
| **India-to-Global** | Global SMBs, mid-market, enterprises served from India | Buys software to replace expensive Western labour | USD/EUR pricing, Indian build and inside-sales cost base |

Tier sizes vary by source. Blume's Indus Valley reports are the usual reference. Do not quote a size without naming the source.

## 2. The 5 friction filters

Grade each PASS, AT RISK, or FAIL, with one line of reasoning.

### Filter 1: The ₹12,000 employee and WhatsApp test
An Indian MSME owner often pays a junior employee or family member ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 a month (rule of thumb) to handle billing, follow-ups and errands on WhatsApp and Excel.
**Test:** Does the product do something that person plus a WhatsApp group cannot? If the owner buys it, can they cut that cost or earn clearly more?

### Filter 2: The hard-money anchor
Standalone software is often seen as "should be free". To collect money reliably, the product must anchor to at least one of these:
1. **Dhandha**: brings new orders or leads.
2. **Chori / bachat**: stops leakage, such as cash pilferage, inventory theft or vendor overbilling.
3. **Sarkari compliance**: avoids GST notices, tax penalties, regulator fines or licence loss.
4. **Hardware or credit bundle**: rides on a device (POS, soundbox, CCTV) or a working-capital line.

**Test:** Which anchor, and how strong? "Saves time" alone is a FAIL outside India 1.

### Filter 3: The gatekeeper (CA, munim, manager, family elder)
In Indian MSMEs and families, the user is rarely the only decision-maker. The CA, munim or store manager controls the daily data. If the product exposes someone's commission, adds typing work, or threatens the CA's role, they quietly sabotage it ("sir, software mein galti aa rahi hai").
**Test:** Who loses power or income if this is adopted, and how do they become a paid channel partner instead?

### Filter 4: Collection and payment friction
- **Consumer:** recurring UPI AutoPay mandates can be paused or cancelled by the user from their UPI app, so monthly subscriptions leak.
- **B2B:** invoices often stretch to 60 to 180 days (rule of thumb), with TDS withheld.

**Test:** Does the revenue model survive mandate churn and long collection cycles? Reframes include annual upfront payment, prepaid credits, per-transaction fees, or deduction at source.

### Filter 5: Public rails and incumbent bundling
India has public digital infrastructure (UPI, ONDC, Account Aggregator, DigiLocker, ABDM, GSTN) and distribution giants (WhatsApp Business, Tally, Zoho, PhonePe, Paytm, Jio).
**Test:** Does the product ride these rails as a wedge, or build a toll booth that a public rail or a giant can give away free?

## 3. The 4 scalability bottlenecks

Grade the idea HIGH (no wall in sight), CAPPED (a clear ceiling; fine as a small profitable business or with an expansion path), or BROKEN (economics get worse as it grows). Name the bottleneck.

1. **The metro bubble.** "1% of 1.4 billion" is not a market. If the product needs English fluency and spare monthly income, the real Indian market is a small fraction of that headcount. Estimate the realistic year-3 domestic revenue. If it is small, the answer is either a lean profitable company or India-to-Global.
2. **USD cost, INR revenue.** As a startup moves from metro early adopters to Tier-2 and SMB users, revenue per user tends to fall while USD cloud and AI costs and paid acquisition costs rise. Does contribution margin per user stay positive outside the top metros?
3. **Linear human ops.** Cheap labour hides broken product flows behind people doing manual onboarding, WhatsApp chasing and data entry. To grow revenue 10x, does ops headcount also grow about 10x? If yes, it is a services business with a software story.
4. **Pin code, language and state walls.** What works in Bengaluru or Gurugram can break in Surat, Indore or Patna. Vernacular UX, local trade associations, state-level permits, and COD return-to-origin fraud all bite.

## 4. Gaps the Chair should name

If the brief lacks the facts to grade a filter, grade it AT RISK, write "unknown: <what is missing>", and make that missing fact a question for the founder in the verdict.
