# Evidence ladder

Place the founder's evidence on this ladder in the brief. Levels 0 and 1 are never "validation". The verdict's 7-day jugaad test should aim to move the founder up at least one level.

| Level | Name | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Founder intuition | "I think kirana owners will love this." |
| 1 | The polite Indian nod | Friends or interviewed shopkeepers saying "haan bhaiya, bahut accha hai, jab ban jaye toh batana". Conflict-avoidance. Worth ₹0. |
| 2 | Market signal | People already pay a CA, agency or employee for this; active complaints; working competitors charging money. |
| 3 | Behavioural switch | Users hand over real data or workflow (GST data, WhatsApp orders, ledgers) to a manual or concierge version. |
| 4 | Retention without discounts | Users come back and complete the core task in week 3 with no cashback, coupon or reward. |
| 5 | Hard commitment | An upfront UPI payment, an annual cheque, or a paid pilot deposit. |

## The 7-day jugaad test

Every verdict that is not DON'T DO IT ends with one test the founder can run this week:
- under ₹5,000;
- zero code where possible (WhatsApp, Google Forms, a spreadsheet, a phone, the founder's own hands);
- aimed at the riskiest assumption the council found;
- with a pass mark stated up front ("7 of 20 shops pay ₹500 upfront").
