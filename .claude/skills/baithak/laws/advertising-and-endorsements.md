## What it says
The CCPA notified guidelines on 9 June 2022 under section 18 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. An advertisement must not be misleading, and an endorsement must not be deceptive. An endorser must do due diligence and rely on adequate information or experience with the product. Endorsers must disclose any material connection with the brand, such as payment or free products. The Department of Consumer Affairs issued Endorsement Know-hows on 20 January 2023 for celebrities, influencers and virtual influencers. They call for clear, prominent disclosure.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the business model depends on endorsements the endorser did not genuinely give, or on claims the advertiser cannot back with evidence.
- GRAY if: you pay influencers and the disclosure label or placement is unclear, or a claim is strong but not clearly false. The line on "misleading" is fact-specific.
- Compliant route: write only claims you can substantiate, brief influencers on material-connection disclosure, and label paid posts clearly and in the language of the post.

## Watch for
- The official CCPA and Endorsement Know-hows PDFs and the PIB releases could not be opened (access refused). Clause numbers and exact label wording are not verified here.
- Penalty figures differ. Secondary sources give up to Rs 10 lakh for a first breach and up to Rs 50 lakh after, and an endorser bar of up to one year, extendable to three years. Check the notified text before quoting.
- Some commentary says the Endorsement Know-hows set no express penalties. Treat enforcement detail as unverified.
- ASCI codes (self-regulation) are not covered in this file. They were not researched.
