## What it says
Only regulated entities may lend: commercial banks, co-operative banks, NBFCs (including housing finance companies) and all-India financial institutions. A lending service provider (LSP) is an agent of a regulated entity that carries out some of its digital lending functions. A default loss guarantee (DLG) covers the lender's losses from borrower defaults. It cannot exceed 5% of the total amount disbursed out of the loan portfolio at any given time. A DLG provider must be a company under the Companies Act, 2013. The lender must invoke the DLG within a maximum overdue period of 120 days, unless the borrower clears the dues first. DLGs are barred for revolving credit offered through digital channels and for credit cards.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the startup lends from its own balance sheet without being a regulated lender, or takes guarantee cover above the 5% cap.
- GRAY if: the product only matches borrowers with a regulated lender, the startup takes a fee and no default risk. Get a lawyer's view on the fee and data-sharing terms.
- Compliant route: operate as an LSP for a regulated lender. Keep any DLG within the 5% cap, with a company as provider.

## Watch for
- Checked in the official RBI text on 2026-11-10: the 5% cap and base (para 23(i)), company rule (para 18(i)), 120-day rule (para 26(i)), and card and revolving-credit bar (para 20(i)).
- Commencement: para 2(ii) says the Directions take effect immediately, except para 6 (1 November 2025). The DLG chapter is not excepted. One read also named para 17 (15 June 2025). Check the RBI text.
- Not checked in official text: the LSP's website disclosure duty, and any rule on loan money passing through a third-party account.
