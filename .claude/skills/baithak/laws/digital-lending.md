## What it says
Only regulated entities (banks, NBFCs, housing finance companies and all-India financial institutions) may lend. A lending app or a tech company may act only as a lending service provider (LSP) for a regulated entity. A default loss guarantee (DLG) is a contract in which another party covers the lender's losses from defaults, up to a set share of the loan portfolio. The 2025 Directions cap that cover at 5% of the amount disbursed under the portfolio (sources differ on the exact base, "disbursed" or "portfolio"). DLG providers must be companies under the Companies Act. A DLG must be invoked within 120 days of default. DLGs are not allowed on revolving credit or credit cards. Digital lending arrangements must be disclosed on the LSP's website.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the startup lends from its own balance sheet without being a licensed lender; it takes uncapped first-loss guarantees from partners; or loan money passes through the startup's or a third party's account instead of going directly between borrower and lender.
- GRAY if: the product only matches borrowers with an RBI-regulated lender and the startup is paid a fee and takes no default risk. Get a lawyer's view on the fee and data-sharing terms.
- Compliant route: operate as an LSP for a registered NBFC or bank. Loan funds move directly between the borrower and the regulated lender's account. Any guarantee stays within the 5% cap.

## Watch for
- The 5% cap is confirmed by several secondary sources and an RBI index entry for "Cap on DLG". The exact wording was not read in the official RBI text, so verify the figure and its base before relying on it.
- Some provisions took effect on different dates (for example, 15 June 2025 and 1 November 2025). Check the RBI text for the applicable date.
- Deposit-taking and pooling of users' money are covered separately in `deposits-and-pooled-investment.md`.
