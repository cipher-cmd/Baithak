## What it says
The Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 defines a "drug" to include medicines and any substance, other than food, intended to affect the structure or any function of the human body (section 3(b)). A "cosmetic" is an article applied to the body for cleansing, beautifying or altering appearance (section 3(aaa)). The Cosmetics Rules, 2020 require registration before import and a manufacturing licence. The Medical Devices Rules, 2017 license devices by risk class.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the product is sold as a medicine or claims to treat or prevent disease without a drug licence. Also if a cosmetic is imported before registration, or a device is made or imported unlicensed.
- GRAY if: the product is a supplement that claims to change how the body works. Clause (b)(ii) treats such a non-food substance as a drug. Claims and labels decide the category, not the name.
- Compliant route:
  - Cosmetics: manufacturing licence from the State Licensing Authority (COS-5 application, COS-8 licence). Import: Rule 12 registration, COS-2 certificate.
  - Devices: Class A and B from the State Licensing Authority (MD-3, MD-5). Class C and D from the Central Licensing Authority (MD-7, MD-9). Imports via MD-14 and MD-15.
  - Supplements: FSSAI rules, not opened here.

## Watch for
- The official Gazette texts could not be read. Form numbers come from summaries. Check the Gazette first.
- One secondary source says Class A and B devices need central classification before a state licence (from 2025). Confirm.
- Class A non-sterile, non-measuring devices reportedly need only voluntary registration. Single source.
- Section 3(aaa) may have changed under the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026. Not confirmed.
- A government panel on moving nutraceuticals from FSSAI to CDSCO was reported. Not verified.
