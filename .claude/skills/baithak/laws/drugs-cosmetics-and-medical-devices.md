## What it says
A "drug" includes medicines for diagnosis, treatment, mitigation or prevention of disease (section 3(b)(i)). Clause 3(b)(ii) adds non-food substances meant to affect the body's structure or function, but only those the Central Government specifies by Gazette notification. A "cosmetic" is an article applied to the body for cleansing, beautifying or altering appearance (section 3(aaa)). The Cosmetics Rules, 2020 require import registration and a licence to manufacture. The Medical Devices Rules, 2017 (in force from 1 January 2018) sort devices into Classes A to D.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: a medicine, or a product claiming to treat or prevent disease, is sold without a drug licence. Also unregistered cosmetic imports and unlicensed devices.
- GRAY if: a supplement claims to change body function and no clause 3(b)(ii) notification covers it. Labels decide the category.
- Compliant route:
  - Cosmetics: manufacturing licence from the State Licensing Authority (Form COS-5, licence COS-8). Import: registration (Form COS-1, certificate COS-2).
  - Devices: Class A and B from the State (Form MD-3, licence MD-5). Class C and D from the Centre (MD-7, MD-9). Imports: MD-14 application, MD-15 licence.
  - Non-sterile, non-measuring Class A devices register online (rules 19G, 19H) and need no manufacturing licence.
