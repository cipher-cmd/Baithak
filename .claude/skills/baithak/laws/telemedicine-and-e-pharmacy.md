## What it says
The Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (2020) form Appendix 5 to the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002. Only a registered medical practitioner (RMP), registered under the Indian Medical Council Act, 1956, may give telemedicine consultations. Practitioners must complete a mandatory course, which may be online, within three years of the guidelines.

**E-pharmacy.** A 2018 draft amendment to the Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 would require registration of online pharmacies and bar online sales of narcotic and psychotropic drugs. Sources checked earlier say the draft was not notified as of May 2026.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: an app issues a prescription, or effectively issues one, without an RMP reviewing and signing it.
- BLACK if: the app sells prescription (Schedule H, H1 or X) medicines online without a licensed pharmacy and a valid prescription process. Get a lawyer's view on the current e-pharmacy position before building.
- GRAY if: the product sells non-prescription items, or the e-pharmacy status is unclear. Also GRAY: a health-data app with no prescribing, which must still meet DPDP rules.
- Compliant route: AI as a clinical assistant for a human RMP, who reviews and signs every prescription. For medicine sales, partner with a licensed pharmacy and follow the current rules.
