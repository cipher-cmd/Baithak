## What it says
Section 79 of the IT Act protects an "intermediary" (a platform that hosts or passes on others' content) from liability for that content, if it meets its due-diligence duties under the IT Rules. The IT Rules, 2021 set those duties. MeitY amended them on 10 February 2026. The amendment defines "synthetically generated information" (SGI, Rule 2(1)(wa)), requires labelling and embedded provenance metadata for it, and requires takedowns within three hours in some cases. Non-compliance can cost safe harbour. Sources disagree on whether that loss is automatic. A later change (a continuous-label requirement, Rule 3(3)(a)(ii), amended 21 April 2026) is reported but its finalisation was not confirmed.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the product lets users publish AI or synthetic content (images, voice, video) of real people without labels, provenance data, or a takedown process, and it is an intermediary with a public audience. Also BLACK: generating deepfakes of real people for endorsement or impersonation.
- GRAY if: the product is an AI tool that generates content for the user's own private use, or a platform that hosts user posts. The duties are new and their application is not yet tested.
- Compliant route: label all AI-generated output, keep provenance metadata, have a named grievance officer and a fast takedown process, and check each new rule before launch.

## Watch for
- The final text of the 2026 amendment (the continuous-label point), and the 21 April 2026 change. Check the MeitY gazette.
- Whether safe harbour is forfeited automatically or only after non-compliance is shown. Sources differ.
- Personality-rights orders from the Delhi High Court (for example, the Aman Gupta order, May 2026) also affect AI likeness. Not a statute, so not covered by this file.
