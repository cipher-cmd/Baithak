## What it says
The 2024 Ministry of Education guidelines bar enrolment of students under 16. Centres must register. A pro-rata refund of fees for the unused period is due within 10 days when a student leaves midway. The CCPA's 2022 misleading-ad guidelines require claims to be provable. Only UGC-recognised or UGC-entitled institutions may award online degrees.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: you award or sell a degree without UGC status. Online degrees need the awarding university to be UGC-entitled (through the DEB) or recognised for online mode. Make no claim of being a university you are not.
- GRAY if: you run a coaching product and the state has not adopted the 2024 guidelines. Check your state's status before relying on them.
- Compliant route: partner with a UGC-entitled institution and show its DEB-ID. Publish refund terms. Refund unused fees pro-rata within 10 days. Back every result, rank or job claim with evidence you can produce.

## Watch for
- Official guideline text was not opened. Penalty amounts and refund wording were not verified. Sources differ on fines.
- A 45-day refund figure in some search results refers to a Karnataka draft, not the Union guidelines.
- Misleading-ad fines: Legal500 says up to Rs 10 lakh, then Rs 50 lakh for repeats. Khaitan says Rs 10 to 50 lakh. Check the CCPA text.
- Enforcement: CCPA fined Byju's IAS Rs 10 lakh (November 2023) under Section 2(28) of the 2019 Act. CCPA fined 24 coaching institutes Rs 77.6 lakh (March 2025). Refund complaints recovered Rs 1.56 crore.
- UGC: the "recognised vs entitled" status and counts (113 entitled, 13 recognised, 2025-26) come from a secondary article. The franchise ban was not verified.
