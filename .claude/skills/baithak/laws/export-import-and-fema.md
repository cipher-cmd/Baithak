## What it says
The Importer Exporter Code (IEC) is a ten-digit DGFT number. Anyone trading goods as an importer or exporter needs one. It is applied for online on the DGFT portal (Form ANF-2A) for a Rs 500 fee. It must be updated each year between 1 April and 30 June, or it is deactivated and blocks all trade. Exporters must realise export proceeds within a set period. Under the amendment notified on 22 September 2026, that period is 9 months for goods and services, and 12 months for exports invoiced or settled in rupees. Companies taking foreign investment must file Form FC-GPR through RBI's FIRMS portal within 30 days of share allotment.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: you ship goods without an IEC, or receive foreign investment and never file FC-GPR. Missed deadlines count as contraventions.
- GRAY if: you export services only. Whether you need an IEC for services was not confirmed. Also, an authorised dealer (AD) bank can extend the realisation period on request, but each bank sets its own policy.
- Compliant route: get the IEC before the first shipment or first foreign payment. Keep the export declaration for each shipment. Collect export money within 9 months, or 12 months for rupee invoices. File FC-GPR within 30 days of allotment.

## Watch for
- Realisation period conflict. The January 2026 text says 15 months. SCC Online and Legal500 report the cut to 9 months, effective 1 October 2026. The RBI notification (FEMA 23(R)/(1)/2026-RB) is listed on rbi.org.in, but its PDF could not be opened. Confirm with your bank.
