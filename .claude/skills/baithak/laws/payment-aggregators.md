## What it says
A payment aggregator (PA) collects money from customers on behalf of merchants. The RBI Directions of 15 September 2025 set the rules. A bank does not need authorisation to run PA business. A non-bank must seek authorisation from the RBI. A non-bank PA must hold merchant funds in a separate escrow account with a scheduled commercial bank in India. A PA may not carry out marketplace business. Settlement timing is set by the agreement between the PA and the merchant, and that agreement must state the timelines.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the startup receives buyers' money into its own account and pays sellers later, without a licensed PA. The same applies to any app that holds users' money in a pool.
- GRAY if: the product only shows a payment link or checkout that routes money through a licensed PA, and the startup never touches the funds. Confirm the PA's name in the contract.
- Compliant route: integrate a licensed PA's split-settlement or marketplace product, so funds never reach the startup's own account.

## Watch for
- Non-bank PA-P entities had to apply for authorisation by 31 December 2025 (para 5(d)(iii)). Check the RBI list of authorised PAs before relying on any date.
- The RBI page shows the Directions as updated on 11 November 2026. Re-check the page before any build.
- A search summary said the consolidated master direction was later withdrawn and replaced by entity-specific instructions on 28 November 2025. Not verified; check the RBI site.
