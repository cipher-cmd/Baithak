## What it says
A payment aggregator (PA) collects money from customers on behalf of merchants and settles it to them later. The RBI Directions of 15 September 2025 consolidate all earlier PA rules. Non-bank PAs need RBI authorisation. They must do full KYC and due diligence on each merchant. The money they collect must sit in an escrow account with a scheduled commercial bank. That account may not be used for, or mixed with, the company's own business. Settlement from the customer's payment to the escrow account must happen on a T+0 or T+1 basis. Marketplaces that offer PA services had to separate that business from the marketplace and apply for authorisation (the 2020 rules set 30 June 2021).

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the startup receives buyers' money into its own current account and pays sellers later. The same applies to any app that holds users' money in a pool without a licensed PA, or that settles funds through a company account.
- GRAY if: the product only gives a payment link or a checkout that routes money through a licensed PA, and the startup never touches the funds. Confirm the PA's name in the contract.
- Compliant route: integrate a licensed PA's split-settlement or marketplace product, so funds never reach the startup's own account.

## Watch for
- Sources differ on the deadline for non-bank PAs to apply for authorisation (31 December 2025, with wind-down by 28 February 2026 for those who did not apply). Check the current RBI list of authorised PAs before relying on any date.
- The 2025 Directions may not have replaced every earlier rule. Check the RBI master direction text before building.
- Official RBI text was not opened in this check; only law-firm summaries and the Sarafpartners note were read.
