## What it says
1. **Unregulated deposits.** The Banning of Unregulated Deposit Schemes Act, 2019 bans schemes that take public money with a promise to repay, outside regulated routes. Its long title excludes deposits taken in the ordinary course of business.
2. **Collective investment schemes.** Under Section 11AA of the SEBI Act, a scheme pooling money from several investors, managed by someone else, with an expected return, needs SEBI registration. Not read in official text.
3. **P2P lending.** The RBI's NBFC-P2P Directions, 2025 (28 November 2025) allow only registered NBFC-P2P platforms to match lenders and borrowers. A platform may not lend from its own funds, take deposits, bear credit risk or give guarantees. Lenders must declare they understand the risk, including loss of the whole principal. Lender exposure across platforms is capped at Rs 50 lakh; borrower total at Rs 10 lakh. Maximum tenor is 36 months.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: users give money to the startup, or pool it in an app, expecting a return. Examples: a rent-deposit fund paying a yield; a student pool with promised returns; a platform lending one user's money to another without an NBFC-P2P licence.
- BLACK if: the startup holds pooled money and decides where it goes, even with no promised return, and the arrangement fits the CIS test. Get a lawyer's view.
- GRAY if: money sits only in a regulated escrow or bank account for a fee, users keep control, and no return is promised.
- Compliant route: partner with a bank, a registered NBFC-P2P (as intermediary), or a SEBI-registered fund or broker. Keep the startup's role to software and referrals.

## Watch for
- BUDS Act section 2 exemptions and SEBI Act Section 11AA were not read in official text (India Code returned HTTP 403 and 504). Verify before relying on them.
- P2P caps, risk rules and tenor were checked in the official RBI notification (paras 22, 26, 27, 29, 46).
