## What it says
Three rules matter for a startup that takes money from many people.

1. **Unregulated deposits.** The Banning of Unregulated Deposit Schemes Act, 2019 bans schemes that take money from the public with a promise to repay, outside the regulated routes. Deposits taken in the ordinary course of business are excluded. Other exemptions were not confirmed in this check.
2. **Collective investment schemes.** Under Section 11AA of the SEBI Act, a scheme in which money from several investors is pooled and managed by someone else, with an expectation of returns, counts as a collective investment scheme (CIS). A CIS needs SEBI registration. Mutual funds, chit funds and cooperative societies are outside the definition. A 2014 proviso deems pooling of Rs 100 crore or more under an unregistered scheme to be a CIS.
3. **P2P lending.** The RBI's NBFC-P2P Directions, 2025 (issued 28 November 2025) allow only registered NBFC-P2P platforms to match lenders and borrowers. Those platforms are intermediaries only. They may not lend from their own balance sheet, take deposits, bear credit risk or give guarantees. Lenders must sign a risk declaration. Sources report a lender exposure cap of Rs 50 lakh across all platforms (from a 2024 report, not yet confirmed in the 2025 text).

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: users give money to the startup (or pool it in an app) and are promised a return. Examples: a rent-deposit fund that pays a yield; a student pool that invests together and promises returns; a platform that lends one user's money to another without an NBFC-P2P licence.
- BLACK if: the startup holds pooled money and decides where it goes, even with no promised return, if the arrangement fits the CIS test. Get a lawyer's view.
- GRAY if: money is only held in a regulated escrow or a bank account for a fee, with users keeping control and no promised return.
- Compliant route: partner with a bank, a registered NBFC-P2P (as an intermediary, with the lender's declaration), or a SEBI-registered fund or broker. Keep the startup's role to software and referrals, with no pooled money.

## Watch for
- The exact exemption list in the Deposits Act (only the ordinary-course-of-business exclusion was confirmed) and the current text of Section 11AA, which sources word differently.
- The borrower and lender caps in the 2025 NBFC-P2P Directions. The Rs 50 lakh figure is from a 2024 report and is not confirmed.
- Verify against the official texts (financialservices.gov.in, sebi.gov.in, rbi.org.in) before relying on any number.
