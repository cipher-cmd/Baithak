## What it says
Under section 16, the Central Government may, by order in the Official Gazette and after consulting the Bureau, make an Indian Standard compulsory for listed goods or services. Section 17(1) bars anyone from manufacturing, importing, distributing, selling, hiring, leasing, storing or exhibiting such goods without the Standard Mark, unless they hold a valid licence. Section 29(3) makes a breach of section 17 punishable with up to two years in prison, a fine of at least Rs 2 lakh for a first offence (at least Rs 5 lakh for later ones), up to ten times the value of the goods, or both. Assent was on 21 March 2016. The Act commences on a date the Centre notifies (section 1(3)); that date was not checked.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: you sell, import, store or display a product listed in a QCO or compulsory registration order without a BIS licence or registration. This covers marketplace sellers and importers, not only factories.
- GRAY if: you are unsure whether your product is listed. Some orders start months after they are published. The line ministry can grant exemptions.
- Compliant route: check the product's order on the BIS website before you import or list it. Buy only from a licence holder, or get the BIS licence or registration yourself.

## Watch for
- Act text opened from a Gazette of India copy (22 March 2016) hosted at thc.nic.in. India Code was unreachable today.
- QCO texts were not opened today. Search results report that many QCOs were revoked or revised in 2025. Check each order's current status on bis.gov.in before relying on it.
