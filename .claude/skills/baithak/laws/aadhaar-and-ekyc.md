## What it says
Only entities licensed by UIDAI can run Aadhaar authentication or eKYC. These are AUAs (authentication user agencies) and KUAs (KYC user agencies). Sub-KUAs use a KUA's eKYC service. On 26 September 2018 the Supreme Court struck down the words "or any contract to this effect" in Section 57, which had let private bodies use Aadhaar under contract. Section 8A allows offline verification. Section 8A(4) bars an offline verification-seeking entity from subjecting the holder to authentication, and from collecting, using or storing an Aadhaar number or biometric information for any purpose. The 2025 Amendment Regulations (uploaded 12 December 2025) add offline face verification and Aadhaar Verifiable Credentials. They require UIDAI registration for entities that want paperless offline e-KYC or verifiable credentials, and they require verification logs to be kept. They set no retention period.

## What it means for a founder
- BLACK if: the startup stores Aadhaar numbers, or runs its own Aadhaar authentication or eKYC without a UIDAI licence or an approved sub-KUA route.
- GRAY if: the product uses offline verification (QR, paperless offline e-KYC, e-Aadhaar or paper) or a digital ID wallet such as DigiLocker, and does not collect, use or store the Aadhaar number or biometrics. Paperless offline e-KYC and verifiable credentials need UIDAI registration. Get a lawyer's view on retention.
- Compliant route: use a licensed KUA or AUA as a sub-KUA, use DigiLocker, or use offline verification without collecting or storing the number.

## Watch for
- Official text was not opened for the Act or the Regulations. The UIDAI listing confirms the 2025 amendment exists, but its PDF was not readable here. The Act wording comes from advocatekhoj.com, a bare-act site.
- The exact retention period after the 2025 amendment. None was found.
- The sub-AUA and sub-KUA rules rest on secondary summaries. UIDAI Circular 03 of 2023 could not be read.
- Overlap with DPDP obligations in `dpdp-act-2023-and-rules-2025.md`.
