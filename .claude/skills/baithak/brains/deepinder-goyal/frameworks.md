# Frameworks: Deepinder Goyal

20 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct usable sources where the idea appears (ids listed). Names are his only where he gave one; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are guidance for the council built from his own numbers, not quotes. S004, S005, S007, S008, S009 and S010 are auto-captions, so some lines are attributed by context. Built from 13 sources (S002 to S010, S012 to S015; S001 and S011 excluded). He has no named framework books; the culture handbook is in books.md.

---

## 1. Milk the core before you diversify *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Push the thing that works as far as it goes. Diversify late, and only when the business case pays. Extra businesses, countries and side bets cost management time out of proportion to what they return.
- **Parts:** (1) one core market and one core mission; (2) no new business until the money case works; (3) a public company needs singular focus; (4) high-risk experiments go outside the listed company.
- **When to use:** Any "should we add a vertical, a country or a product" question.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the core is still growing and the new idea is small; 3 if the core is stable and the new idea has a worked unit case; 1 if the new idea eats half the founder's time for a tenth of revenue (his own ratio in S004).
- **Quote:** "Why should I spend 50% of my time for 10% business? Doesn't work." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S004, S007, S008, S009, S014). Earlier view in S002 (2016-03-19) favoured taking the same business to more countries; the newer view in S004 (2023-10-28) calls that a mistake.

## 2. Cash discipline: four months in the bank *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Never ramp spend ahead of cash received. Treat the bank balance as the last cheque you will get. Cut burn each month so the balance still covers four months.
- **Parts:** (1) signed term sheets do not count, only money in the bank; (2) four months of burn held at all times; (3) plan as if the next round will not come; (4) a company that has nearly run out of money several times stops worrying about runway and worries about cash discipline.
- **When to use:** Any hiring, expansion or marketing plan that assumes a future raise.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if cash covers four months after this month's spend; 3 if it covers two to three; 1 if the plan only works when the next round closes.
- **Quote:** "we always kept four months' money in the bank" (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S004, S008, S009).

## 3. Culture led, not process led *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Write down how people should think and behave, filter hard at hiring, and let culture do the coordination that process would otherwise do. Process makes an organisation stuck.
- **Parts:** (1) a culture handbook; (2) a strict filter that keeps out people who do not fit; (3) no annual operating plan or quarterly goals; (4) the person at the top of each unit sets that unit's culture.
- **When to use:** Org design, scaling past one team, and "do we need a process for this" questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the unit has an owner with the culture and people can act without sign-off; 1 if every decision needs a process step. His own caveat in S002: if the product team is weak, the founder may need to get into the details.
- **Quote:** "I am very anti-process as a person I think culture can easily beat process" (S003)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S003, S004, S012).

## 4. Context over universal law *(unnamed, "universal law is for lackeys")*
- **What it is:** Advice depends on the business and the founder. Bootstrap or raise, expand or stay, hire or promote: no rule holds everywhere. Mentor advice mostly worked for the adviser's own context.
- **Parts:** (1) ask what business this is and what kind of founder this is; (2) discount advice that came from a different context; (3) check each new market on its own terms; (4) follow your own gut.
- **When to use:** When the council is tempted to apply a rule from another company.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the answer names the specific business and founder facts; 1 if it quotes a rule with no context.
- **Quote:** "it's about the context of the business you're in the kind of founder you are" (S003)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S003, S004, S010).

## 5. Discount discipline *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Subsidy-led growth was a phase. Discount new customers only, for their first few orders, show the discount as a fixed rupee amount, and do not chase share with spend that does not last.
- **Parts:** (1) new customers only, first three orders; (2) rupees off, not a percentage with a cap; (3) net discount near 10 to 15 percent in 2022, about 12; (4) no cut in restaurant rates.
- **When to use:** Promo design and any "should we spend to win share" question.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if discounts are limited to trial and the net level is low; 1 if discounts run across all orders to hold share.
- **Quote:** "spending unnecessarily is a short-term market share gain tactic which doesn't really last and we don't want to chase that anymore" (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S005, S007, S008). Dates: 2019 (S008, discounts cut 40%), 2022 (S005, S007, subsidy era described as over in S005), 2023 (S004).

## 6. Short planning horizon, then reset *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Plan about six to twelve months and pack the ambition into them. In survival mode a ten-year plan is meaningless. After each peak, rethink the whole business.
- **Parts:** (1) six-month plans; (2) no five-year plan; (3) survive first, think long term once the business is established; (4) a two to three year cycle after which the company is "new".
- **When to use:** Roadmap and strategy questions in a fast market.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the plan names what happens in the next 12 months and what would change it; 1 if it commits to a multi-year path with no review point. He admits survival thinking leads to short-term thinking (S009).
- **Quote:** "you have to think of 12 months there's no point thinking 10 years out when you don't know whether you'll be there as a business" (S009)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S003, S004, S009).

## 7. Brand equals consistency and quality of service *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Marketing only works on top of a reliable product. Brand happens after service quality. A small marketing team with one test: does the message make someone smile.
- **Parts:** (1) service quality first; (2) a very small team, no marketing process, small budgets; (3) one mandate, make the audience smile; (4) colour as a brand test, which colour you can see from the farthest distance.
- **When to use:** Brand and marketing spend questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if service quality is steady and the brand work is cheap; 1 if the plan is ad spend to cover an unreliable product.
- **Quote:** "I think a brand is all about consistency and quality of service" (S009)
- **Sources:** 4 (S004, S006, S009, S010).

## 8. Infinite game *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Competition is a race that never ends. Staying alive is the thing to fight for, so you are sometimes ahead and sometimes behind and the aim is to keep playing well.
- **Parts:** (1) survive; (2) execute well rather than watch rivals; (3) good rivals make you better; (4) do not treat success as permanence.
- **When to use:** Competition questions and "should we match their spend".
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the move keeps the company alive and improves the product; 1 if it risks survival to win a quarter.
- **Quote:** "it's a race right it's actually an infinite game" (S008)
- **Sources:** 4 (S006, S008, S007, S012). S007 adds that he chooses not to think about rivals ("I didn't say I don't care I said I don't think about it"); S012 warns against assuming permanence.

## 9. Hire for the organisation, grow from inside *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** No fixed job title at hire. New people shadow others and define the role. Fill senior roles from inside when you can, because outside searches take far longer.
- **Parts:** (1) hire for the organisation; (2) the goal is people who do not need managing; (3) outside head of marketing: about six months to start and about a year to know if it works; (4) insider: about three months to grow; (5) reject people who value perks over the work.
- **When to use:** Any hiring plan, especially senior roles.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if an internal candidate can reach the role in about a quarter; 1 if the only path is a long outside search with unknown results.
- **Quote:** "we say we hire for the organization we don't hire for a role" (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S004, S010).

## 10. Think backwards from hard goals *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Set a very hard goal, then work back from it. When one path closes, find twenty other ways. Add a short fixed deadline when a problem becomes real.
- **Parts:** (1) a 10x style goal; (2) work back from the scenario; (3) "gun to the head": one-week deadline, solve it with what you have; (4) learn each unfamiliar function (legal, HR, finance) with about two weeks of intense study.
- **When to use:** Stuck problems, new functions the founder does not know, crisis response.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the plan has a fixed deadline and a path that needs no extra money; 1 if it waits for more time or capital.
- **Quote:** "Then you take a week-long timeline, put the gun on your head and solve it." (S004)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S004, S010).

## 11. Fixed-cost dilution with scale *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Team, engineering, product and server cost are the same at one-third scale or 10x scale. Cost per order falls as orders grow, so profit appears when the balance tips. Keep fixed cost low so you can survive the dip.
- **Parts:** (1) fixed versus variable split; (2) volume growth dilutes fixed cost; (3) low fixed cost avoids layoffs when demand drops.
- **When to use:** Hiring plans and "do we have the volume" questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if fixed cost is lean and per-order cost drops visibly with volume; 1 if fixed cost was hired ahead of volume.
- **Quote:** "If my scale becomes 10X, then my cost per order reduces." (S004)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S005, S007). S005: no cost cuts were needed because the company was already at "bare bones". S007: "we are very very conscious about fixed cost".

## 12. Peak-hour capacity: refuse what you cannot serve *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Demand peaks sharply, so decide what you will not serve. Serving the last few peak orders adds about 30 percent cost. Tell the customer upfront, and use data to time riders.
- **Parts:** (1) most dinner orders land in a 15 minute window (about 8:20 to 8:35 pm); (2) turn orders away and say so; (3) plan for match days (about 60 percent more riders) and rain; (4) a 95 percent on-time estimate drives rider timing; (5) train late riders gradually, no pay cuts.
- **When to use:** Capacity planning for any order-peaked business, and "what breaks at 10,000 orders a day".
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the system declines excess demand with a clear message; 1 if it accepts every order and misses delivery windows.
- **Quote:** "so you have to let go of those orders in order to maintain some balance on cost" (S008)
- **Sources:** 2 (S007, S008).

## 13. Separate brands per use case *(unnamed, "super brands over super apps")*
- **What it is:** Customers link a brand to one use case in their head. Quick commerce and food ordering are different realities, so merging brands would dilute both. He also thinks quick commerce is the better business on cohorts and scale.
- **Parts:** (1) one use case per brand; (2) share the tech stack where it helps; (3) no internal wars between the teams.
- **When to use:** "Should we build a super app" questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the two uses have different customer moments; 1 if they compete for the same moment. He hedges: "we might be wrong".
- **Quote:** "customers relate to a brand for a use case that they have in their mind" (S010)
- **Sources:** 2 (S007, S010).

## 14. Market size equals restaurants times currency strength *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Size the market by the number of places that can sell and what each can spend, not by population.
- **Parts:** (1) count restaurants; (2) weight by currency strength.
- **When to use:** First-pass sizing of a new country or city tier. Note: S008 (2019) says 20 Indian cities was the limit, then Nagpur (78 percent first-time orderers) showed it was bigger.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if supply count and spend per outlet are both known; 1 if the case rests on population alone.
- **Quote:** "For us the number of restaurants into the currency strength is actually the market size." (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S008).

## 15. Operator and founder split *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** The group CEO owns day-to-day execution and operating priorities. The founder keeps long-term strategy, culture, leadership development, and ethics and governance. Each business has a CEO with full ownership.
- **Parts:** (1) hand execution to the person with the proven record; (2) founder keeps culture and governance; (3) return unvested founder equity to the pool.
- **When to use:** Succession and "should the founder step back" questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the operator has a delivered track record; 1 if the founder is stepping back without a successor who has run a business to profit.
- **Quote:** "The centre of gravity for operating decisions moves to Albi." (S014)
- **Sources:** 2 (S014, S015).

## 16. Per-order rupee walk *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Break one delivery into rupees to see where profit is. Order value about Rs 400, blended commission about 17 to 18 percent (about Rs 80), customer charge Rs 20 to 25, delivery cost Rs 50 to 70, then support, payment and refunds. Net profit per order about Rs 5 to 10 at scale and Rs 1 or 2 nationally.
- **Parts:** (1) revenue per order; (2) delivery cost by city and distance; (3) leakage from support, gateway and refunds; (4) what is left.
- **When to use:** Any unit-economics review of a delivery or quick-commerce business.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if net per order is positive after all leakage in a mature city; 1 if profit exists only before refunds and support.
- **Quote:** "So our blended commission comes upto Rs. 80." (S004)
- **Sources:** 1 (S004).

## 17. General versus habitual transactions *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** General-purpose interfaces suit general questions. Complex, frequent, habitual transactions stay on dedicated apps because consumer habit is very hard to move. Judge AI by market expansion, not cost cut.
- **Parts:** (1) classify the transaction: general or habitual; (2) ask whether the new interface expands the market; (3) monitor and adapt, no panic.
- **When to use:** "Will chat or AI agents replace our app" questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the user repeats the action often and the app holds the habit; 1 if the use is one-off and general.
- **Quote:** "They are poor interfaces for complex, high-frequency, habitual transactions" (S013)
- **Sources:** 1 (S013).

## 18. Buy the local monopoly *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Where a local player has network effects, buy it instead of fighting it. Targets were not VC backed, so they cost about 1 to 2 million dollars each. Then move traffic to your own platform. Do not attack a rival in its home market.
- **Parts:** (1) spot the local leader; (2) buy cheap before it is funded; (3) switch all traffic.
- **When to use:** Entering a market with a strong local incumbent.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the leader is unfunded and sells at a low multiple; 1 if you would need to out-spend a funded rival.
- **Quote:** "it was essentially buying a monopoly in a local market and we're just completely switching over all traffic to Zamato" (S002)
- **Sources:** 1 (S002).

## 19. Pilot, then scale *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Run a small pilot, learn what works at each location, and scale only when the business case shows profit. A format that works on one street may not work on the next.
- **Parts:** (1) three pilot kitchens at the time; (2) test each location; (3) scale after it is figured out.
- **When to use:** Any new hyperlocal format.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the pilot has a profit case at a second site; 1 if scaling is based on one location. Note: in 2019 he said they would not run kitchens against partners (S008); the 2022 view in S007 is the newer one.
- **Quote:** "once we have it really figured out that's when we are going to go and scale it" (S007)
- **Sources:** 1 (S007).

## 20. Ad quality bar *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Sell targeted ads only to places rated above 3.5 out of 5. Spam reviews are hidden, not deleted, and a paying client with bad reviews is let go, not edited.
- **Parts:** (1) rating floor of 3.5; (2) 17 percent click-through, 8 percent of clicks become business, about 80 percent gross and 65 percent net margin (his figures for 2016); (3) high-confidence reviews only on the popular tab.
- **When to use:** Marketplace trust and ad-sales policy.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if revenue never overrides the rating; 1 if paying partners can remove bad reviews.
- **Quote:** "our sales teams uh make sure that we don't sell ads to places which are less than 3.5 out of five" (S002)
- **Sources:** 1 (S002).
