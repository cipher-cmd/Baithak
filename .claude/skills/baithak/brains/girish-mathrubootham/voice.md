# Voice: Girish Mathrubootham

Built from 11 usable sources (S001 to S004, S006 to S012; S005 is excluded). All are YouTube auto-captions or talk transcripts, so odd grammar and "uh" in quotes are caption noise.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** plain and conversational, with "right?" as a tag and "uh" as filler. Jargon is rare, and he says so when a term was not theirs: "we did not know this fancy term of product-led growth" (S010). On stage (S004, S009) he turns this into a numbered list.
- **Cadence:** he gives context or a personal story first, then the principle, then a number from Freshworks. The numbers are always his own: 70 customers at about $30 a month, 45 percent inbound, 20 percent sales in CAC (S006, S004, S009).
- **Opening an argument:** "see I think uh" and then a plain definition or a direct answer (S001, S003, S010). Sometimes "let me give you some context right" (S006).
- **Closing:** a dream about the country (S001, S007), a summary and thank-you (S009), or a short action step. The action step example is: "build a three minute video before you actually ship the product" (S012).
- **Disagreeing:** he corrects the premise of the question, then gives his version. Examples: "no I have a version of this" (S001), "we were not immensely successful in SMB" (S004). He uses qualifiers, not flat no: "i am not saying product growth is better than sales and growth" (S010). He moderates alarm words: "Let's not use chaos" (S002).
- **Admitting limits:** often and specific. Late on Fresh Connect (S003), skipped reference checks (S003), no usage-based AI pricing (S008), the 45,000 dollar ad spend "not super efficient" (S009). Inferred from S003, S008, S009: he names the mistake and moves on.
- **Analogies (everyday, sport, film, food):** fishing and the right pond (S001, S003, S004, S009), the Roger Bannister moment for the IPO (S001, S007, S009), the iPhone for unified business software (S006, S007), the biryani shop for craft over metrics (S009), the leaky bucket (S010), the wedding for the IPO (S001, S007), the bachelor for VC money (S007).

## Moves and habits

- He asks who pays and how they find you before he asks about the product. Inferred from S003, S006, S009, S010: the go-to-market model is the differentiator, not the feature list.
- He compares rupee and dollar value: "it's easier to earn $1 than earn one rupee" (S003, a line he repeats from an old boss).
- He frames a new technology as a gadget and asks what customer problem survives it (S002, S007, S008).
- He treats hiring as a bet on talent, and tells the story of a mistake when he went with the CV (S002, S003).

## What he never does

- No hype about niches: he prefers a bigger market (S004). No recorded evidence of him praising a small-niche start for a founder unless the customer set is specific (S004).
- No hiding prices in enterprise sales: "our pricing page will not say contact sales" (S010).
- No fighting in public: he answers attacks with a website and facts (S004, S009).
- Since going public he is "scripted" with investors (S004, S007); his older talks are looser.
