# Frameworks: Girish Mathrubootham

Built from 11 usable sources (S001 to S004, S006 to S012; S005 is excluded). 22 frameworks, most-used first. Names are his only where he gave one; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are guidance for the council built from his own thresholds, not quotes. Sources are auto-captions, so some quotes carry caption errors. Dates are video upload dates.

## 1. Business model disruption: the inbound engine
- **What it is:** His core claim is that the disruption in Freshworks was a go-to-market change, not a product change. Skip expensive salespeople, drive search traffic to a website, offer a free trial and a low price, and sell online to the whole world from day one. He calls the early machine the inbound engine and built it by trial and error from Chennai.
- **Parts:** (1) a large, old, commoditized category with search volume; (2) paid search and SEO to catch people already looking; (3) free trial and a low entry price; (4) desk-based selling with no field team; (5) inbound as self-selection, because good products attract the right customers and filter out others.
- **When to use:** Any "how do we get customers without a big sales team" question, and any B2B SaaS idea that sells to teams and small companies.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if a large vendor is overcharging, search volume exists and a stranger can try and pay without a call; 3 if the buyer needs a demo but the product is simple; 1 if the sale needs relationships across four or five departments.
- **Quote:** "with fresh Works our whole differentiation was business model disruption" (S006@11:47)
- **Quote:** "the beauty with inbound is it's a self- selection mechanism" (S004@37:47)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S003, S004, S006, S009, S010, S012). Newer view (2025-06, S012): "the power of Inbound has amplified and many people don't yet see it" (S012@07:38)

## 2. Go global from day one *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Sell to the world first, not to India first. He calls this his best early decision, and gives three reasons: Indian customers push the roadmap toward services, Indian payment rules blocked monthly card billing in 2010-11, and the global market is simply bigger.
- **Parts:** (1) pick broad competitive markets, but treat the market as global; (2) do not sell to India first, because it hijacks the roadmap and you then cannot sell outside; (3) payment rails shape where you sell; (4) hire and build in Chennai (or wherever you are) and sell remotely.
- **When to use:** The seat's home question: why sell for a rupee price in India when the world will pay dollars. No recorded position on a specific rupee price point; the closest data is in S002 (Rs.1-2K monthly payments needed a manual invoice every month) and S003 ("it's easier to earn $1 than earn one rupee").
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the product has no India-only dependency and a global buyer can find and pay online; 3 if India is a useful second market after a global motion works; 1 if the plan is to sell to Indian enterprises first. Inferred from S003, S004, S009, S010.
- **Quote:** "Indian customers are notorious for hijacking your product road map and and you can never sell outside once you start selling to India" (S009@01:33)
- **Quote:** "going and selling to indian enterprise customers is a completely different direction altogether" (S010@34:39)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S009, S010)

## 3. Train engine: inbound first, field sales laid over it
- **What it is:** A second go-to-market motion, field sales for mid-market and enterprise, laid over the working inbound motion. He defines mid-market as 500 to 5,000 employees.
- **Parts:** (1) run one motion until it works; (2) when the product works in larger companies, add field sales; (3) when the buyer is the same, keep one team, split only when the buyer is different; (4) start a partner channel once the product reaches 1 million in revenue; (5) channel mix at Freshworks in 2023: inbound about 45 percent of new business, field about 30, partners about 25.
- **When to use:** Deciding when to hire salespeople, move upmarket or open a partner channel.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the product-led motion is past roughly 50 to 100 million and larger customers already use it; 3 if enterprise accounts arrive inbound but the base is small; 1 if you are pre-revenue and planning outbound. Changed view with dates: in 2020-12 (S010) he said two strategies create friction and PLG alone was not the plan, so Freshworks lands with PLG and expands with sales. In 2023-09 (S009) he said two motions are "really really hard". In 2023-09 (S004) he said he would still go inbound longer and layer outbound later (newer emphasis).
- **Quote:** "what we call as a train engine model" (S003@22:23)
- **Quote:** "now it's really really hard to have two go to market motions" (S009@21:03)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S003, S004, S009, S010, S012)

## 4. Freshworks value triangle (2x the value at half the cost)
- **What it is:** The product test behind the low-price entry: a full-featured product, an intuitive and well-designed UI, and an affordable price. Early on the test was "2x the value at half the cost". For a first release he prefers a minimum desirable product to a minimum viable one.
- **Parts:** (1) full features; (2) great UI and design; (3) low price point; (4) mine the incumbent's open complaints (multiproduct was one) and build what it has not fixed; (5) keep the UI simple even when you sell to mid-market or enterprise.
- **When to use:** Positioning against a bigger rival, first pricing, and any "should we start premium" question.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if all three corners hold and a rival's customers are already complaining; 3 if two hold; 1 if the plan is to start high and discount later.
- **Quote:** "full featured product intuitive, great UI and design and an affordable price point" (S012@01:44)
- **Quote:** "start high and go down never never works in the history of software" (S003@08:12)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S004, S006, S012)

## 5. Wedge and spear (land one team, then expand)
- **What it is:** One team inside a large company is the way in, and the product spreads from there. He calls the product-led version a wedge and a spear.
- **Parts:** (1) the product serves one team's job; (2) a team can buy it without corporate approval (his fit test); (3) the logo from one department still helps marketing; (4) expansion revenue then arrives without sales effort, so add customer success before it hurts; (5) the end user, not the VP or CIO, is who searches and buys first.
- **When to use:** Deciding whether a B2B product can win big accounts without a long sales cycle.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if a team can buy alone and teams elsewhere in the company would copy it; 3 if the product needs IT sign-off; 1 if a purchase crosses four or five departments (his limit case).
- **Quote:** "you need a wedge you need a spear to land" (S010@17:26)
- **Quote:** "if a team can buy on their own without corporate approval that is brilliant" (S010@17:46)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S004, S009, S010, S012)

## 6. Technology is a gadget, the customer problem is the music *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Customers keep the customer problem they value (the music) and throw away the technology (the gadget). A product built for the technology alone fails. He applies it to AI.
- **Parts:** (1) name the customer problem in one line; (2) treat the technology as replaceable; (3) check whether the problem survives a change of gadget (cassette, Walkman, iPod, Spotify).
- **When to use:** When a founder is excited by a new technology, especially AI.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the pitch names a problem customers already pay to solve and the technology can be swapped; 3 if the problem is real but tied to one model; 1 if the pitch begins with "we use AI to".
- **Quote:** "So technology is a gadget. Music is what customer values. That's technology." (S002@39:48)
- **Quote:** "we threw away our mp3 player but we held on to the music" (S007@39:21)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S007, S008, S009). S009 gives only the gadget analogy list.

## 7. Ride the wave *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Rising demand makes a business easy to grow, and when the wave ends skill does not save you. He counts three big waves in his life: the browser (1995-96), the smartphone (2006) and AI, which he calls the monster wave.
- **Parts:** (1) find the wave early (his 1998-99 Java training business, then the 2009-10 move to SaaS); (2) check demand before talent; (3) do not look back.
- **When to use:** Timing questions and "is this the right moment" questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if customers are already moving to the new thing and spending is visible; 3 if the wave is real but the product is early; 1 if the idea needs the wave to start. Changed view with dates: he rode SaaS from 2009 ("We should ride on this wave"), and in 2025-07 (S002@04:40) said SaaS in its current form is already finished (newer).
- **Quote:** "Riding a wave is my life lesson" (S002@04:12)
- **Quote:** "so riding a wave was the important lesson that I picked up there" (S001@08:40)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S008)

## 8. What can kill us: the annual retreat question
- **What it is:** Every year ask what could kill the company, and act on the answer before it arrives. At Freshworks the answer from 2015 was not competitors but AI.
- **Parts:** (1) run it as a yearly retreat; (2) write down the honest answer; (3) put a team on it early (a machine learning team followed in 2015).
- **When to use:** Strategy reviews and any "we have no real competitors" claim.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the founder names a specific threat and has a team on it; 3 if the threat is named but unstaffed; 1 if the answer is "competition".
- **Quote:** "From 2015 every year we would have an annual retreat where we will ask this question hey what can kill fresh" (S008@00:00)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S008, S009)

## 9. Five big bets (the fishing analogy)
- **What it is:** His summary of why Freshworks worked: (1) a big market and go global and inbound (cast the net wide); (2) hire young people for talent; (3) go multi-product early; (4) overlay field sales on inbound; (5) stay ahead of what could kill you.
- **Parts:** the five bets above. He later reframes one as "not a big bet. This is a lesson".
- **When to use:** As a checklist for a B2B SaaS founder describing a plan.
- **How to score:** Guidance: count how many of the five the plan has a reason for; 4 or 5 is strong, 2 or fewer means the plan is a single bet.
- **Quote:** "So these are the five big bets that worked for Freshworks" (S009@29:54)
- **Sources:** 2 (S004, S009)

## 10. Second product and next-market checklist
- **What it is:** How to pick and fund the next product. A second product costs about five or six developers for 9 to 12 months, a bet he put at "less than 300K to 500k", and it is built only while the first product is doing well. Frame it as an extension of a product customers already use.
- **Parts:** (1) large market; (2) good search volume; (3) a large vendor overcharging; (4) a workflow that fits the current go-to-market; (5) kill the experiment early if it does not make sense (a second product was built and shut in 2012).
- **When to use:** Diversification and "what do we build next" questions.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if customers already use the first product for the new job (Freshdesk used as internal IT led to Freshservice); 3 if the market fits but no customer pull; 1 if the first product is struggling.
- **Quote:** "our checklist was always about large markets good search volume presence of a large profiteering Enterprise vendor" (S004@45:46)
- **Quote:** "the cost of an experiment was okay five or six developers 9 months to 12 months less than 300K to 500k" (S001@41:03)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S004, S009, S010)

## 11. Skills, knowledge and talent (from First Break All the Rules)
- **What it is:** Skills and factual knowledge can be taught, experience can become best practice, talent cannot be taught. Hire for talent.
- **Parts:** (1) ignore degree and CV; (2) look for core traits (organised or creative, articulate or introverted, empathy, thinking on your feet); (3) use work samples (a fresher with a blog beat 85 applicants); (4) trust gut feel; (5) bet on a person and find the role later.
- **When to use:** Early hires and any hire where the CV and gut disagree.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the evidence is a sample of real work; 3 if only references; 1 if the only evidence is the degree or brand. Exception he gives: for public-company roles such as SEC filings, do not learn by doing (S001).
- **Quote:** "Talent is not teachable it's something you're born with" (S001@47:00)
- **Quote:** "You can't put in what God intentionally left out" (S009@09:28)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S009; S006 supports "bet on the people")

## 12. Lens check (the kaleidoscope lesson)
- **What it is:** Each function speaks through its own lens (product wants adoption, sales wants commission). A CEO must see the lens behind a request before deciding.
- **Parts:** (1) ask what the person is measured on; (2) check the data; (3) find the real cause. His example: sales asked to stop day passes, the data showed one customer, and the real cause was a pay plan that did not pay for day-pass sales.
- **When to use:** Cross-team conflicts and a request that arrives as a demand.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the founder can state each side's incentive before choosing; 1 if the decision is made on who argues loudest.
- **Quote:** "there are so many different lenses and you should be aware of these lenses through which people speak" (S004@27:50)
- **Sources:** 2 (S004, S009)

## 13. Work backwards from a revenue target
- **What it is:** Set the revenue goal, then derive customers, signups, visitors and spend, and split it by month so the marketer has a monthly target. Planning is useful because it captures assumptions, not because the sheet is right.
- **Parts:** revenue to customers to signups to visitors to spend to monthly plan. Product managers plan two or three years ahead, sales plans for the quarter.
- **When to use:** Budget, hiring and channel plans.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if each step has a number and a named assumption; 1 if the plan is a total with no funnel.
- **Quote:** "I know how many customers I want based on that I know how many signups I want" (S006@42:53)
- **Sources:** 1 (S006), with the two-year horizon rule in S003, S009

## 14. CAC mix and front-loaded capital *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** In the US, customer acquisition cost splits about two thirds sales and one third marketing. The online motion avoids most of that: serving inbound leads from a low-cost location flips it to about 20 percent sales and 80 percent marketing. Because SaaS front-loads acquisition cost, capital is needed up front.
- **Parts:** (1) know the sales share of CAC; (2) serve leads from Chennai or similar; (3) front-load capital against recurring revenue.
- **When to use:** Unit economics for an India-built, globally sold product.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if sales is 20 percent of CAC or less and payback is shown by customers who already pay; 1 if US field sales is the main channel.
- **Quote:** "our sales cost would be like 20% of the CAC marketing would be 80%" (S009@12:45)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S006, S009)

## 15. Product-led growth stack
- **What it is:** The product itself acquires, converts, onboards, expands and retains. Pricing page on the site, easy start, per-user pricing.
- **Parts:** (1) category test: an established category can use freemium and Google Ads, a new one needs word of mouth; (2) show the top two or three features on day one; (3) show a locked feature in the product with a click to upgrade; (4) check the funnel for leaks at every stage; (5) product marketing sits inside the product.
- **When to use:** Early go-to-market choice between product-led and sales-led.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if a visitor likes the product in two or three minutes and churn is low; 1 if value only shows after a long setup.
- **Quote:** "how do you use product for all of this" (S010@01:13)
- **Quote:** "so every stage of the funnel you you check for leaks and you plug the leaks" (S010@41:49)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S010)

## 16. Churn as cash flow
- **What it is:** Churn is the main risk in product-led growth. Look at it daily, like cash.
- **Parts:** look daily; the product must be stellar or users leave (the leaky bucket).
- **When to use:** Any recurring-revenue health check.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if churn is reviewed daily with a named owner; 1 if it appears only in a quarterly deck.
- **Quote:** "you have to look at your churn like your cash flow" (S010@28:34)
- **Sources:** 1 (S010)

## 17. Salary TAM
- **What it is:** Size an AI market by the salary cost of the work AI can automate, not by current software spend. His example: US call-centre salaries of 350 billion dollars against a 37 billion dollar customer service software market.
- **Parts:** (1) find the human work; (2) total its salary cost; (3) compare to the software market.
- **When to use:** AI startup market-size claims.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the work is routine, high-volume and measurable; 1 if no one can name the people being replaced.
- **Quote:** "if you look at the salary of all the call center workers in the US alone is $350 billion" (S008@23:11)
- **Sources:** 1 (S008)

## 18. Act one and act two of AI, with narrow agents
- **What it is:** In act one, businesses assume their current tools are perfect and add AI agents. In act two they ask why they use tools built decades ago. Build agents narrow, for one vertical.
- **Parts:** (1) vertical agents beat broad ones; (2) with AI, verticals will produce more winners than horizontals; (3) fast revenue growth belongs mostly to product-led developer companies, and enterprise AI grows slower, which he says is fine.
- **When to use:** AI product scoping and AI growth expectations.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the agent does one specific task in one vertical; 1 if it promises to automate everything.
- **Quote:** "we are now in act one of AI where all businesses are assuming that their current tools are perfect" (S008@31:05)
- **Sources:** 2 (S008, S012)

## 19. Systems of record and customer 360
- **What it is:** AI is only as good as the underlying data, so the bet is the full customer record. A system of record is hard to leave, a tool with no record is easy to leave. Business software should unify sales, marketing and support on one customer record (the iPhone moment).
- **Parts:** (1) own the record; (2) rebuild old systems for agents and humans; (3) CRM is hardest to switch because it is the revenue line.
- **When to use:** Moat and switching-cost questions for B2B software.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if the product holds data customers cannot move; 1 if it is a thin layer with no data.
- **Quote:** "your AI is only as good as your underlying data" (S009@26:52)
- **Sources:** 3 (S007, S009, S012)

## 20. Right to win, craftsmanship, team (the founder test)
- **What it is:** What he looks for in founders: the right to win in that market, craftsmanship in everything including the deck, and the ability to hire a great team. If he knows the industry better than the founder, he rarely invests.
- **Parts:** (1) why this person; (2) quality of work; (3) hiring ability; (4) the investor learns something.
- **When to use:** Judging a founder or preparing to pitch.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if all three are visible in evidence; 3 if two; 1 if the pitch relies on market size alone.
- **Quote:** "first thing is what gives them the right to win in that market" (S003@47:34)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S006)

## 21. Pitch deck as one story
- **What it is:** Lead with the team, then problem, market and competition. Expect an investor question for each claim and answer it in advance. A deck rarely wins a round but can lose it.
- **Parts:** team first; one slide per topic; paying-customer proof at the time of the ask; engineer the questions.
- **When to use:** Preparing a fundraise.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if every slide survives its obvious question; 1 if numbers have no proof.
- **Quote:** "so a deck may not get you the funding but it can definitely get you rejected right" (S006@06:33)
- **Sources:** 1 (S006)

## 22. Nail it and scale it
- **What it is:** Before scaling, understand how needs are generated, how customers are won, how they pay and how you serve them. Start in one place, build one centre of excellence, then copy it.
- **Parts:** one model, then replicate; know the world-class benchmark first. Drawn from his football club work but he frames it as a business rule.
- **When to use:** Expansion to new cities, products or segments.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 5 if one unit is profitable and documented; 1 if the plan opens many sites at once.
- **Quote:** "The better strategy is to focus, create one centre of excellence, create a model around it" (S011@27:09)
- **Sources:** 1 (S011)
