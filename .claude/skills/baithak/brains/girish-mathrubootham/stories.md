# Stories: Girish Mathrubootham

16 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Built from S001 to S004, S006 to S012 (S005 is excluded). Most-used first. Source ids in parentheses. Lines without quotation marks are paraphrase.

1. **Cisco and the department logo.** One Cisco team bought 10 seats, Cisco was counted as the 30,000th customer, and later Procter and Gamble asked to evaluate the product. Lesson: one team's buy opens a bigger account, and a small logo still helps marketing. "that's the beauty of product link growth is it helps you land those things" (S010@36:20). Also S004, S009.
2. **The $45,000 channel test.** After winning the Microsoft BizSpark India challenge with 40,000 dollars and no equity, he spent about 45,000 dollars in two months on Google AdWords, LinkedIn and smaller channels and reached 70 customers paying about 30 dollars a month. That helped raise the first round. Lesson: spend early to find the channels that scale. "Not super efficient at that time, but I think overall it played out well" (S009@06:21). Also S004, S006. S004's captions misstate the spend as 445,000, so the figure here follows S009.
3. **Selling from Chennai, remotely.** The first customers came inbound and he did not meet one in person until about 100 customers, and then only to meet Deep Kalra. Burger King took 300 seats, closed on the phone. Lesson: an inbound model needs no field sales early. (S001, S004, S006, S009). The first paying customer paid 38 dollars after 2.5 hours in the product (S006).
4. **Hacker News and the Zendesk price move.** A Hacker News post about Zendesk raising prices pushed him to start. His March 2011 post then went to the top page, and the team reached 100 customers in 100 days. Lesson: a rival's price move opens a door, and public writing brings in investors. (S001, S003).
5. **The Zendesk attack.** After a funding announcement in 2011 he was attacked online as a copy. The company answered with a website exposing a paid blogger, and the community backed it on Hacker News. Lesson: do not fight in public, show the facts. "instead of fighting on Twitter we responded with the website" (S009@29:13). Also S004.
6. **The day-pass scare.** Sales wanted day passes stopped. The data showed one customer, and the real cause was a sales plan that did not pay for day-pass deals. Lesson: find the lens behind the request before acting. (S004, S009).
7. **Freshservice from internal IT.** Customers used Freshdesk as an internal help desk, so he built a purpose-built ITSM product, which became a product worth about 300 million dollars. Fresh Marketer was killed in 2012 on investor advice. Lesson: frame the next product as an extension of one that works. (S003, S009, S010).
8. **Fresh Connect lost to Slack and Teams.** It took more than two years to launch, and CIOs wanted one tool, not a third. He conceded the category. "so I think we were late and we lost uh that game" (S003@34:30). Lesson: adoption shows fast whether a product works.
9. **Solar City, one team to thirteen.** A 25-seat deal grew until 13 teams used over a thousand licences, about a million dollars a year under 13 invoices, which the CFO did not see in the top 10 vendors. Lesson: land one team and let it spread. (S003).
10. **The annual "what can kill us" retreat.** From 2015 the leadership asked each year what could kill Freshworks. The answer was AI, not rivals, and a machine learning team followed. Lesson: name the existential threat, ask it every year. "From 2015 every year we would have an annual retreat where we will ask this question hey what can kill fresh" (S008@00:00). Also S009.
11. **Hiring a fresher over 85 applicants.** For a marketing role he chose a fresher with a strong blog over experienced candidates. With the Ruby on Rails team, no hire knew Ruby until employee 69. Lesson: samples and talent beat resumes. "till employee number 69 we did not hire a single programmer who actually knew Ruby on Rails" (S009@10:09). Also S004.
12. **The resume that backfired.** He went with a strong resume against his gut and it failed. Related: skipped reference checks on investor referrals, and skipped hiring enough engineers in 2015 when sales outgrew engineering. Lesson: trust the gut, check references, plan hiring a year ahead. (S002, S003).
13. **Java training and the dotcom bust.** In 2001 no student wanted Java, so demand decided, not talent. Friends had put money in and he kept the debt as a moral duty. Lesson: ride a rising wave and repay people who trusted you. "so riding a wave was the important lesson that I picked up there" (S001@08:40). Also S002, S011.
14. **Payments and the RBI rule.** In 2010 to 11 Indian card rules blocked automatic monthly billing, so he raised monthly invoices for Rs 1 to 2K payments. This was a first reason to sell abroad. Lesson: the payment rails shape where you sell. (S002).
15. **The Dindigul biryani shop.** A small-town restaurant's second generation grew it to 25 to 30 stores by protecting the recipe. Lesson: the craft is the foundation, and metrics belong to the board, not the staff. "the foundation of the business is in the biryani" (S009@18:31). Also S004.
16. **No deck, just 70 customers and a TAM he refused to build.** At the first raise he had 70 paying customers at an average of about 30 dollars a month. Asked for a TAM, he cited the rivals' revenues instead, and Shaker Kirani later told him to stop asking founders that. Lesson: customer proof beats a market-size slide. (S001, S006).
