# Principles: Girish Mathrubootham

Built from 11 usable sources (S001 to S004, S006 to S012; S005 is excluded). 26 decision rules, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Sources are auto-captions, so some quotes carry caption errors. Weighted toward B2B SaaS and India-to-global decisions.

---

## 1. Pick one go-to-market motion and scale it before adding another.
- "having two strategies creates a lot of friction" (S010)
- "If you can scale with one go to market Motion, I would say do that all you can" (S012)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S009, S010, S012). **Confidence:** high. Newer view: 2025-06 (S012) is firmer than 2020-12 (S010), when Freshworks already used product-led to land and sales to expand.

## 2. Go global from day one and do not sell to India first.
- "that was probably the best decision that we took in those days of how do we go global from day one" (S009)
- "Indian customers are used to a Services mindset" (S003)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S003, S004, S009, S010). **Confidence:** high.

## 3. Build the second product only while the first is doing well.
- "if you want to build a second and third product you have to do it when things are going good because when things are tough you're going to be asked to cut cost" (S001)
- "so you have to do it when your first product is actually doing well" (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S003, S009). **Confidence:** high.

## 4. Hire for talent, not for degree or CV.
- "I don't care about your degree I want to find what your talent is" (S001)
- "Whenever I went against it and went with the resume, it backfired" (S002)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S009). **Confidence:** high. He admits resume mistakes and skipped reference checks (S003).

## 5. Start with a low price and a basic product, then move up.
- "start high and go down never never works in the history of software" (S003)
- "2x the value at half the cost" (S006)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S006, S012). **Confidence:** high.

## 6. Land one team and let the account spread.
- "make sure you can uh build something that a team can use" (S010)
- "many times what starts out as a 25 seat 50 seat 100 seat deal would expand to several 100 seats" (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S004, S009, S010). **Confidence:** high.

## 7. Add field sales only after the product works in larger companies, then go all in.
- "if you are not worried about growth uh i i'm talking about say after 50 million or 100 million uh so you can actually start going sales growth" (S010)
- "once you pick that, then you go all in and actually do everything" (S012)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S009, S010, S012). **Confidence:** high.

## 8. Go after a bigger market, not a small niche.
- "try to go after a bigger Market it's like increases your chances of success than getting stuck in a very small niche market" (S004)
- Limit he states: for a specific customer set, a niche beachhead makes sense (S004).
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S009). **Confidence:** high.

## 9. Make the product intuitive and well designed, even for enterprise buyers.
- "even if you're building for midmarket Enterprise it doesn't hurt to keep that intuitive uh uh user friendly interface" (S003)
- "product design being world class is table Stakes" (S004)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S004, S010, S012). **Confidence:** high.

## 10. Start from the customer problem, not the technology.
- "Building a product for the sake of technology has always failed" (S002)
- "the core customer problem that needs to be solved which the customer values that is the music" (S008)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S007, S008, S009). **Confidence:** high.

## 11. Plan revenue two years out and zoom out every quarter.
- "you have to be planning for revenue that's going to come 2 years down the line" (S009)
- "you should take a weekend every quarter and just zoom out from the day to day stuff" (S012)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S009, S012). **Confidence:** high.

## 12. Build the company from where you are if you can find the people.
- "we can build a company and be successful from wherever we are" (S004)
- "I was also adamant in hiring and keeping all the jobs in Chennai because that's how you create the talent" (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S004, S007). **Confidence:** high.

## 13. Keep pricing public on the website.
- "so now we make a philosophy that our pricing will be open uh it will be on the website" (S003)
- "our pricing page will not say contact sales the prices will be laid out clearly" (S010)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S010). **Confidence:** medium.

## 14. Watch churn like cash and add customer success before it hurts.
- "you have to look at your churn like your cash flow" (S010)
- "Customer success is very, very important because our expansion revenue is actually happening without us doing anything" (S012)
- **Sources:** 2 (S010, S012). **Confidence:** medium. The company had no customer success team until 2016 (S012).

## 15. Answer an attack with evidence and product, not a public fight.
- "instead of fighting on Twitter we responded with the website" (S009)
- **Sources:** 2 (S004, S009). **Confidence:** medium.

## 16. Raise in parallel, and show paying-customer proof at the ask.
- "Venture hacks had a good advice saying always fundraise in parallel" (S001)
- "by the time we signed the term sheet with AEL we had 70 customers who were paying us an average of $30 a month" (S006)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S006). **Confidence:** medium.

## 17. Be aggressive in the market, not abrasive with people, and no brilliant jerks.
- "you can be aggressive in your work but you should not be abrasive in your relationships with people" (S001)
- "don't be a jerk even if you're brilliant don't be a brilliant jerk" (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S003). **Confidence:** medium.

## 18. Learn at someone else's expense; learning by doing fits only where a mistake is cheap.
- "I would say that a better strategy would be to learn at somebody else's expense" (S011)
- "learning by doing uh may not be the best strategy when you have access to them" (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S011). **Confidence:** medium.

## 19. In the AI era, build a demo and let inbound work; do not hire SDRs for outbound.
- "build a three minute video before you actually ship the product" (S012)
- "it's okay to be an AI native startup even with slower growth if you're going after enterprise clients" (S008)
- **Sources:** 2 (S008, S012). **Confidence:** medium. This is his advice for the current AI moment, which he calls "a moment in time opportunity" (S012).

## 20. Keep eyes and ears in the Valley and set your learning pace at par with AI.
- "I think right now it is important to have your eyes and ears in the valley" (S008)
- "your pace of learning has to be at least at par with AI" (S008)
- **Sources:** 1 (S008). **Confidence:** low.

## 21. Decide with about 70 percent of the data because most decisions are reversible.
- "we need to know how to take decisions with 70 per cent of data" (S011)
- "speed is important, because most decisions are reversible" (S011)
- **Sources:** 1 (S011). **Confidence:** low.

## 22. Spend where it affects product quality and stay frugal elsewhere.
- "but if it's impacting the quality of the final product then those are the areas where you want to spend" (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001). **Confidence:** low. Capital efficiency claim in S010: product-led growth needs less capital.

## 23. Do not buy revenue growth with an acquisition.
- "we are not looking to make any big acquisitions because we don't need to make acquisitions for revenue growth" (S007)
- **Sources:** 1 (S007). **Confidence:** low. Buy only for talent or a go-to-market edge.

## 24. Do not push metrics down to employees; protect the craft.
- "you cannot push down the metrics to the people" (S009)
- **Sources:** 1 (S009). **Confidence:** low.

## 25. Run reference checks, especially on investor referrals.
- "I did not do ref uh uh ref checks on candidates who actually came through like investor referrals" (S003)
- **Sources:** 1 (S003). **Confidence:** low. This is an admitted mistake, turned into a rule.

## 26. Raise money to fund SaaS acquisition, but know VC money is forgiving.
- "which means I need to frontload Capital" (S006)
- "being a vc funded company is like being a bachelor who can party hard" (S007)
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S007). **Confidence:** medium. In S004 he adds that with VC money you say yes more than no, and as a public company you must be clear.
