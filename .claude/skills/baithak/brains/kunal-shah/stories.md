# Stories: Kunal Shah

12 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Built from S001 to S010 notes. Most-used first. Source ids in parentheses. Lines without quotation marks are paraphrase.

1. **Laundry startups against the maid.** Around 800 laundry startups copied a Western idea where maids are costly. In India a maid already does it, and he calls that the Delta 4 option. Lesson: the jugaad is the competitor, so the app needs a 4 point gap over it. (S010, S002)
2. **Truecaller, Uber and the brag test.** Nobody downloaded Truecaller from an ad, everyone from a friend. Railway counter versus IRCTC, Uber and Ola versus Meru, smartphone versus camera are his examples of irreversible Delta 4 shifts. Lesson: if users brag, the gap is big enough. (S010, S001)
3. **The suit test.** Buying a suit online scored 5 against 7 offline, so tech did not beat the old way. Lesson: below 4 the switch is reversible and nobody brags. (S001)
4. **FreeCharge prototype.** As he tells it, he took a printed visual of the idea to brands, operators and consumers, and 95 percent said yes before any coding. Lesson: test an uncoded prototype first. (S010)
5. **Five-star hotel ratchet.** Once you have stayed in a five-star it is very hard to go back to a lower star. Lesson: a better experience resets the baseline for good. (S007)
6. **Rivers and dams.** Founders build clever dams where no river exists. Lesson: check where demand already flows and what people will pay, down to one rupee. (S007, S002, S006)
7. **Living room against bedroom.** A retail buyer found living-room products had about 3x the margin. A renovation firm relaunched as "living room renovation" and got 70 to 80 percent of revenue from it. Lesson: Indians pay for what visitors see. (S002, S004)
8. **Black henna and mixtapes at 15 to 17.** He sold black henna at about 10x with scarcity, and personalised mixtapes at 3 to 4x. Lesson: people pay for differentiation and status, and high price reads as quality. (S002, S003)
9. **Weddings and the suitcase company.** 80 to 83 percent of suitcases are bought for weddings, with about 4.4 million weddings a year, and there is no wedding unicorn. Lesson: founders often do not know their own market, and where people want the process, efficiency tools miss. (S002, S003)
10. **Hindustan Unilever fairness.** A head of skin care put fairness in every cream, and skin care became a large part of profit. Lesson: study where profit is large, because it shows what people pay for. (S007)
11. **Netflix, Spotify and Prime in India, and Meta at $3 or $4 per user.** They came for tens of millions of paying Indians and did not get them. Lesson: user count is not revenue in India. (S001)
12. **CRED's IPL campaign in peak COVID.** He bought it against board advice because his 30 to 40 million creditworthy customers were stuck at home. Lesson: a judgment call on limited data aimed at one cohort. (S003, S007, S008)

Also used: the Indian crab basket for status societies (S004), the orangutan puzzles at Singapore Zoo for ads that are not spoon-fed (S007), prepaid recharge as the first credit product since utilities came before shopping in digital payments (S008), and cultured pearls killing the status of pearls, used to ask about lab-grown diamonds (S001).
