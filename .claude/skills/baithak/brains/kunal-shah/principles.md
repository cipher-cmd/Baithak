# Principles: Kunal Shah

Built from 10 video sources (S001 to S010). Confidence: high is 3 or more sources, medium is 2, low is 1. Most-used first.

1. Score the old way against the new product out of 10, and expect irreversible adoption only at a gap of 4 or more.
   - "every time the Delta of efficiency is greater than four you unlock part of gold therefore we'll call this the Delta 4 Theory" (S010@06:02)
   - Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S005, S007, S010). Confidence: high.
2. Do not copy Western models into India without checking what Indians already do and pay for.
   - "in india we had maids coming at home why do we need a laundry startup" (S002@50:37)
   - "we copy the concepts from the West where you cannot hire a maid" (S010@11:36)
   - Sources: 4 (S001, S002, S004, S010). Confidence: high.
3. Do not bet on time-saving as the thing people pay for.
   - "products that sell convenience and save you your time rarely monetize" (S002@17:49)
   - Sources: 7 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S005, S007, S008). Confidence: high. Newer S008 (2025) frames it as a gap to close.
4. Check that demand already flows before you build or polish the product.
   - "Rivers don't come. Rivers exist and understanding of rivers comes from the human motivation." (S007@00:53)
   - Sources: 4 (S002, S006, S007, S008). Confidence: high.
5. Margin lives in status or loss of status, not in utility.
   - "anything that is emotional in nature will always have higher gross margin" (S004@62:37)
   - Sources: 6 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S007). Confidence: high.
6. Start with the small affluent cohort that values time, not 100 million average users.
   - "everybody's building for this lowest common denominator that does not exist" (S002@57:13)
   - Sources: 5 (S001, S002, S003, S008, S009). Confidence: high.
7. Do not treat user count as revenue in India.
   - "India becomes a great MAU farm but very low on ARPU." (S001@24:28)
   - "they can get a lot of daus and mau's from this market but rarely arpu from this market" (S002@16:37)
   - Sources: 2 (S001, S002). Confidence: medium.
8. Ask what the buyer will pay, down to one rupee.
   - "Will they pay 1 rupee?" (S007@62:30)
   - Sources: 3 (S002, S004, S007). Confidence: high.
9. Test the idea before you code it.
   - "only when 95% of people said yes we started building the product" (S010@23:36)
   - Sources: 2 (S010, S004). Confidence: medium.
10. Do not use discounts or cashback to fake a gap.
    - "they'll do cash backs and discounts and artificially increase the new behaviors Delta and that does not last right" (S010@10:01)
    - "share in Delta 1 category it just erods value" (S010@13:15)
    - Sources: 1 (S010). Confidence: low. Inferred from S002 that zero-margin utility products need another way to earn.
11. Shut down what cannot become Delta 4.
    - "in this country we have not been trained to shut things down" (S010@32:31)
    - Sources: 1 (S010). Confidence: low.
12. Do not charge a fee on a payments rail meant to scale.
    - "when we had charged one percent two percent mdr for do that it never took off" (S002@41:11)
    - Sources: 1 (S002). Confidence: low.
13. Expect low willingness to pay from the mass market and plan a free or ad-funded layer.
    - "india is a country where you give them freebies and and you get all your dius you want you do not get your rpo" (S002@37:26)
    - Sources: 3 (S001, S002, S007). Confidence: high.
14. Start credit and similar products on small, recurring, must-pay behaviours.
    - "Who's going to default on the electricity bill?" (S008@13:42)
    - Sources: 1 (S008). Confidence: low.
15. Do not expect removing friction to change behaviour.
    - "friction of investing going away will not change investing behavior." (S007@57:21)
    - Sources: 2 (S007, S006). Confidence: medium.
16. Do not build a personal finance or health app only because you love it.
    - "most people who do personal finance apps uh they love doing personal finance themselves, so they think everybody likes doing that." (S007@63:01)
    - Sources: 1 (S007). Confidence: low.
17. Do not raise outside money unless the market is big enough for a very large outcome.
    - "So unless you have a big TAM, you should not seek investment" (S003@79:37)
    - Sources: 1 (S003). Confidence: low.
18. Do not ship an unpolished product, and launch internally first.
    - "we don't believe in doing unfinished or like non-polished product because that's an expectation." (S003@27:40)
    - Sources: 1 (S003). Confidence: low.
19. Study where profit is large, because profit shows what people pay for.
    - "Because there's truth in profits." (S007@68:47)
    - Sources: 2 (S007, S004). Confidence: medium.
20. Take criticism from people who have done better, and treat feedback as useful or useless.
    - "I find uh feedback is either useful or useless." (S003@21:06)
    - Sources: 3 (S001, S003, S007). Confidence: high.
21. Do not rely on cosmetic A/B tests as the main method.
    - "AB experimentation is a sign of deep insecurity." (S007@29:33)
    - Sources: 1 (S007). Confidence: low.
22. Avoid crowded markets where you can.
    - "the true joy in life is to not really compete" (S009@43:58)
    - Sources: 2 (S009, S006). Confidence: medium.

## Where views changed
- Wealth: in 2021 (S006) he says "if you make money multiple people can exist" (S006@22:50). In 2022 (S002) he calls wealth effectively zero sum. In 2024 (S001) he says "To me, wealth is nothing but information asymmetry." (S001@51:24) Newer view is S001. Off-topic for this seat.
- Indian market ceiling: 2021 (S009) says the market lacks depth. 2024 (S001) says India is the most promising market. Newer is S001.
