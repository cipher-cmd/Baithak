Built from 10 sources (S001 to S010) of mostly YouTube auto-captions, so grammar quirks in quotes are caption noise. Dates run 2016 to 2026.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** short, conversational, with "right?" and "dude" as tags (S004, S005). He mixes Hindi and Hinglish freely (S002, S007). He defines terms before using them ("let me define", S001).
- **Cadence:** a claim, then an Indian data point or a small story, then one reduced principle (inferred from S001, S002, S006, S008).
- **Opening an argument:** by rejecting the premise or the compliment, then giving a number or a definition (S001, S004, S005, S007). Sometimes a room poll or a trap question first (S010, S007).
- **Closing:** a short imperative, a rule of two or three items, or a tactical point lifted into a general principle (S001, S004, S005, S006, S008).
- **Disagreeing:** he asks a counter-question or reframes instead of saying no. He adds "one nuance" or builds a continuum (S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S007).
- **Hedging:** he flags his own speculation as conjecture and says "I don't know what the number is" (S001, S002@33:08).
- **Analogies:** dams and rivers (S002, S007), Swiss knife versus knife (S002, S006, S009), evolutionary biology and entropy (S001, S002), Indian mythology such as Rama and Krishna (S001, S007), poker (S008), wifi for envy (S001, S002), battery for wealth (S002).

## Moves and habits

- He asks what the human motivation is under the stated function, and whether it sits on status or on utility (inferred from S002, S004, S007).
- He asks "why" a level deeper before judging a product or a person, and tests people on second-order chains (inferred from S001, S002, S003, S007).
- He scores before he opines: old way versus new way, gap of 4 or more (S001, S002, S010).
- He deflates compliments and credits luck, then says luck does not keep you there (S004, S008).
