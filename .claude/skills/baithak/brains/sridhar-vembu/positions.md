# Positions: Sridhar Vembu

36 topics. Built from S001 and S003 to S012 (S002 is excluded and not cited). Political, religious, nationalist, and family material is left out under the seat rule. Each stance has a verbatim quote, a source id, and a date (upload date from `sources.md`). Where his view changed, both versions sit side by side with dates, and the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say "no recorded position".

## Raising outside money
- **Stance:** Do not take outside money that forces an exit. Use patient capital, and fund growth from profit.
- "that's where the seed of never taking outside money" (S001, 2024-04-12). The partners began refusing outside money after a 1999 offer.
- "We definitely need these patient entrepreneurs along with patient capital" (S005, 2025-10-05)
- "we realized we didn't need money" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- **Sources:** S001, S005, S008 (3).

## Bootstrapping, debt and capital
- **Stance:** Earn the money a first VC round would give in the first five years, then reinvest profit in the next product. Company debt: no recorded position. Nearest evidence is the rule that the business must pay its bills from day one (S008).
- "the first five years is to earn enough capital the VC level of money" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- "we could become our own VC and invest in the next thing" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- "everything you do has to be pay the bills" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- Country debt is a separate question: "country has to go into debt that's totally unsustainable" (S001, 2024-04-12).
- **Newer (2026):** he wants money to circulate locally, not in large central flows: "not to create vast capital flows, but make capital flow locally" (S007, 2026-06-19). He describes Indian households' gold as insurance: "we always call it insurance. I never call it investment" (S007, 2026-06-19).
- **Sources:** S001, S005, S007, S008 (4).

## Selling the company and going public
- **Stance:** No planned exit. Exit means the end of the company, so keep it going and keep working on what you enjoy.
- "Exit means only one thing, death." (S005, 2025-10-05)
- "shall we make it a forever we won't sell" (S001, 2024-04-12). The partners kept the company after a large offer.
- "They offered us $25 million. We only had $2 million revenue" (S004, 2025-11-16). He describes the 1999 offer as declined.
- **Going public:** "if you don't go public you cannot acquire easily because most companies will use the stock as their acquisition currency" (S008, 2018-09-05). He chose to stay private then. No recorded position on an IPO since 2018.
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S005, S008 (4).

## Pricing and discounting
- **Stance now:** Charge a real price. Undercharging is a mistake, and the next customer should pay more.
- **Over time:**
  - 2017-11-28: "keep the price very nominal very clean" (S006). Prices were kept low and simple.
  - 2024-04-12: "you could have asked for 10 times the money I would have given it" (S001). A Silicon Valley customer told him he was underpricing.
  - 2025-11-16 (newer): "Make sure you charge the next customer a better price, he told me" (S004). He reports this as advice he received.
- **Discounting:** no recorded position on discounts as a sales tactic. He tells of a $30,000 software deal signed too cheap, and says the customer taught him to ask for more (S004, 2025-11-16).
- **Read:** inferred from S001 and S004 that his view has moved toward higher prices, away from S006.
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S006 (3).

## Sales approach and founder selling
- **Stance:** Let customers discover the product. Do not push it on them. Founders must learn to sell themselves.
- "we'd much rather that you discover it this way and we surprise you than we push it down your throat and you hate us." (S005, 2025-10-05)
- "we have try to extend that same privilege to our customers" (S006, 2017-11-28). This is about letting customers try the product first.
- "you have to learn to sell the first job of entrepreneur" (S001, 2024-04-12)
- "You keep quiet. I'll handle this. You watch." (S004, 2025-11-16). He describes a Silicon Valley salesman who taught him this.
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S005, S006 (4).

## Customer support and refunds
- **Stance:** Keep a human on support. Refund unhappy customers without argument. Give human agents AI help, and do not replace them.
- "if a customer is ever unhappy, we'll refund them the money, no questions asked." (S005, 2025-10-05)
- "we want that customer talking to a human being" (S005, 2025-10-05)
- **AI in support (2025-04):** "We have not aggressively pushed this in Zoho yet because I don't want to upset the customers" (S003, 2025-04-08).
- "we empower the human support agent with AI assistance to help them do their job better" (S010, 2025-11-17)
- **Sources:** S003, S005, S010 (3).

## Customer data and advertising
- **Stance:** No ads in the messaging product, and no selling or third-party access to user data. He accepts lower profit to keep that.
- "we do not intend to monetize ever using advertising or uh third party access to that data" (S011, 2025-10-13)
- "and of course the profit potential would be lower and that's okay" (S011, 2025-10-13)
- "we are not an advertising company and we are a very much a software company" (S011, 2025-10-13). This is his reason: the company is B2B software.
- **Sources:** S011 (1).

## Trust, data location and government reach
- **Stance:** Keep data inside national borders where regulators require it. Do not promise customers that no government can reach their data.
- "we want to jealously guard our data within the nation's boundaries" (S011, 2025-10-13)
- "If you're operating in India, the kill switch cannot be situated somewhere else" (S004, 2025-11-16)
- "Zoho is not here to guarantee you that American government cannot do anything to you" (S011, 2025-10-13)
- **Sources:** S004, S011 (2).

## Focus, niche and diversification
- **Stance:** Enter a small niche with few players, then spread across many products so that no one competitor can end the company.
- "if you find a niche which not a lot of players then easy to find customers" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- "you have to have a diversified mix of business that is something that was clear you cannot be exposed with all eggs in one basket" (S001, 2024-04-12)
- "we never get fazed by first of all because we're so diversified" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- Cross-selling: "once you acquire a customer you are able to sell multiple products into them" (S008, 2018-09-05).
- **Newer (2026):** he is holding off new areas. "I think this is not the time to expand our focus right now." (S012, 2026-10-23). He names metallurgy as his current focus: "metallurgy we are very weak in India. That's one area I'm particularly focused on now" (S007, 2026-06-19).
- **Read:** inferred from S001 and S008 (spread the risk) and S007 and S012 (narrowing now).
- **Sources:** S001, S007, S008, S012 (4).

## Competition
- **Stance:** Do not race rivals. Measure against yourself. Treat claims that one company is the category leader as bubbles.
- "I don't I don't lose sleep over any of it we are enjoying the journey" (S005, 2025-10-05)
- "we are only in a race against ourselves" (S006, 2017-11-28)
- "that whole category leader thing is a bubble that bubble will bust" (S001, 2024-04-12)
- "I expect a very vibrant competition within India" (S004, 2025-11-16)
- **Newer (2026):** the test is global quality, not rivals. "If we are not actually competitive globally, our products are not competitive, nobody has to buy them." (S012, 2026-10-23). He also says "We are winning major deals against Microsoft." (S012, 2026-10-23).
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S005, S006, S008, S012 (6).

## Marketing, ads and brand
- **Stance:** Word of mouth and a good product come first. Heavy sales and marketing spend is a warning sign. A brand is built by holding ground for decades.
- "We like to take care of customers and and word of mouth." (S005, 2025-10-05)
- Spend ratio: "they'll spend 70% sometimes 80% in sales and marketing they'll spend like 10% in R" (S001, 2024-04-12). He rejects a model that spends 70 to 80 percent on sales and marketing and about 10 percent on R&D.
- Cross-promotion instead of ads: "we can now cross promote across our own product" (S008, 2018-09-05).
- **Over time:**
  - 2025-10-13: "we are not a very marketing oriented company" (S011). He says the company has been under-marketed.
  - 2026-06-19 (newer): "but the marketing is the easy one to learn" (S007). The hard part is the brand: "But, what we need to do is uncompromisingly stand the ground" (S007, 2026-06-19). "We have a 50x problem right now" (S007, 2026-06-19).
- **Read:** inferred from S007 and S011 that marketing is treated as learnable and brand as slow.
- **Sources:** S001, S005, S007, S008, S011 (5).

## Hiring for fit and degrees
- **Stance:** Hire for learnability and fit, not for credentials. Grades and degrees do not predict job performance.
- "we disregard formal credentials for the most part" (S006, 2017-11-28)
- "the grades in college the degrees and the job performance don't correlate very well" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- "I say this in every place in India there's talent like this. Everywhere." (S009, 2026-05-21)
- On students: "make sure you have a strong grounding in fundamentals by doing things not by writing exams by only doing well in exams." (S003, 2025-04-08)
- **Sources:** S003, S006, S008, S009 (4).

## Rural sites and local talent
- **Stance:** Put a long personal commitment behind a rural site. Hire from nearby schools and train in-house. Do not manage a rural site from a distance.
- "I'll spend 10 years if necessary that is vital" (S003, 2025-04-08)
- "they have been out of high school only one year they are already doing extremely well" (S003, 2025-04-08). He describes the results of hiring from nearby government schools.
- "you cannot assume that readymade talent will be available in whatever area that you're working on" (S012, 2026-10-23)
- "So, the talent is there." (S009, 2026-05-21). He describes a 30-person team, average age about 20, drawn from local government schools.
- **Read:** inferred from S003, S009, and S012. Talent is present in many places, but a new site has to build it.
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S009, S012 (4).

## Hiring from inside and keeping people
- **Stance:** Promote from inside, hire to keep people, and treat staff as family.
- "I always tell people it'll be somebody who already works here because I don't believe in this whole professionally searching" (S001, 2024-04-12). He expects future leaders to come from within.
- "we hire to keep people" (S006, 2017-11-28)
- Retention: "This is a talent business." (S012, 2026-10-23). Without big acquirers in India, talent does not accumulate. Stated as: "there are not big acquiring companies in India for talent could accumulate like that." (S012, 2026-10-23)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S006, S012 (4).

## Firing and layoffs
- **Stance:** Do not cut people to ride out a downturn. Fire slowly, and move people to a better fit first.
- "I also didn't want to repeat the layoff it's very painful lay of people" (S001, 2024-04-12). After the 2001 bust he let sales staff go, and then set out to build a company that would not need to do that again.
- "we fire slowly I mean we don't we are not a company that is just quick on the trigger" (S006, 2017-11-28)
- "If we expect people to be an extended family, how can we just simply, you know, randomly lay off people" (S004, 2025-11-16)
- **Sources:** S001, S004, S006 (3).

## Services versus products
- **Stance:** Build your own products and intellectual property. Services earn quick money but do not build lasting value.
- "we went in the direction of IT services and we should have gone into products more." (S005, 2025-10-05)
- "But what you should have built, we should have built is the intellectual property." (S005, 2025-10-05)

- **Sources:** S005, S011 (2).

## Build versus buy and own infrastructure
- **Stance:** Build what is not yet solved in India. Make the tools you need. At scale, run your own servers.
- "It's a solved problem in Microsoft. It's not a solved problem in India." (S003, 2025-04-08)
- "if you don't make the tools yourselves, you won't be very good engineer in whatever we do." (S009, 2026-05-21)
- "it is cheaper to run your own servers and your own thing than the public cloud at scale" (S008, 2018-09-05)
- "we are at least 50% cheaper, maybe 70% cheaper in our infrastructure" (S009, 2026-05-21)
- **Sources:** S003, S008, S009 (3).

## Copying and reverse engineering
- **Stance:** Copy first, then innovate. Learn by taking products apart and studying the best ones.
- "If you don't know how to copy, you can never actually innovate" (S007, 2026-06-19)
- "Take apart a drone, a Chinese made drone. See how it's done" (S007, 2026-06-19)
- A Japanese lens maker that does not sell the grinding machines it uses is told as a story (S009).
- **Sources:** S007 (1 quote), S009 (story).

## Manufacturing and factories
- **Stance:** Prototype first with a small team, then buy machines and open the factory after two or three iterations. A small factory costs a few crore, not billions.
- "You can set up a motor factory, small motors, with about 6 to less than 10 crore investment" (S007, 2026-06-19)
- "Not just manufacturing, not just buy the machinery and open a factory, but also look into the process, manufacturing process" (S009, 2026-05-21)
- "Sometimes it's better to import also used machinery because they still retain value, we can learn from it, and then we can upgrade to better machinery as our workforce is better" (S007, 2026-06-19)
- Design and prototyping can be small: "10 20 people with computers with some machines for prototyping that can do it that requires very little friction" (S003, 2025-04-08)
- **Read:** inferred from S003, S007, and S009. He says the factory stage is where approvals and friction start (S003).
- **Sources:** S003, S007, S009 (3).

## Government schemes and revenue mix
- **Stance:** Do not build the company on government money. Keep the government share of revenue small.
- "Because last thing we need is one more government scheme on this" (S007, 2026-06-19)
- "Our percentage of government revenue is actually small." (S012, 2026-10-23)
- "the percentage of revenue coming from the government, particularly the Indian government, is actually very low" (S011, 2025-10-13)
- On innovation policy: "Nowhere in the world, not even in China, is the government inventing the technologies" (S004, 2025-11-16). He says the private sector invents and government supports.
- **Sources:** S004, S007, S011, S012 (4).

- "Learn to build products. Then we'll get the export once we have a decent quality" (S007, 2026-06-19)
- "90% of our revenue is coming from the global markets" (S011, 2025-10-13)
- "We are winning major deals against Microsoft." (S012, 2026-10-23)
- **Sources:** S007, S011, S012 (3).

## Quality before revenue, consumer apps and B2C
- **Stance:** For a new consumer product, get it right before you monetise. Go into B2C only with a strong foundation or a lot of capital, and plan for a 10-year horizon.
- "revenue is not a current priority" (S012, 2026-10-23)
- "We want to make sure the app is perfect, bulletproof." (S012, 2026-10-23)
- "we are actually doing a lot of user group testing right now uh with non-tech people." (S012, 2026-10-23). Testing comes before integrations.
- "you better have some strong either foundation from a a company like Zoho" (S012, 2026-10-23)
- "We should not overhype anything including Arata Zoho or whatever" (S011, 2025-10-13)
- **Sources:** S011, S012 (2).

## Hardware and semiconductors
- **Stance:** Making beats trading. Not now for chips.
- "There is no semiconductor trading. It is all making." (S005, 2025-10-05)
- "When a chip is made, a government that controls a fab can insert a back door on the chip" (S011, 2025-10-13). He uses this to argue for institutional trust.
- **Newer (2026):** "I think this is not the time to expand our focus right now." (S012, 2026-10-23). The 2025 view named making as the value; the 2026 view puts hardware on hold.
- **Sources:** S005, S011, S012 (3).
