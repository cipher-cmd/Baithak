# Principles: Sridhar Vembu

24 decision rules, grouped by theme. Built from 11 sources (S001, S003 to S012); S002 is excluded. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule (ids listed). Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1. Where his views changed or conflict, the dates are given and the newer view is marked.

## Money and ownership

1. **Stay self-funded, and refuse money or terms that force an exit.** Outside money and exit clauses pull the company away from its customers.
   - "I said because I don't want to exit" (S008@11:58)
   - "Exit means only one thing, death." (S005@12:52)
   - Sources: 4 (S001, S004, S005, S008). Confidence: high.

2. **Survive first, then grow.** Pay the bills from the first day, and do not treat growth as the goal.
   - "if we survived we would thrive long term" (S008@01:00)
   - "I never actually wanted to grow tack that way" (S006@28:52)
   - Sources: 2 (S006, S008). Confidence: medium.

3. **Expect a long road, and measure yourself by your own yardstick.** Other companies' metrics are not the test, and rivals are not the race.
   - "what other companies treat as metrics has never been relevant to us" (S005@14:02)
   - "we are only in a race against ourselves" (S006@30:14)
   - Sources: 4 (S001, S005, S006, S008). Confidence: high.

4. **Spread the risk across many products, and do not sell into a bubble.** A single product or a single boom should not be able to sink the company. In 1999 he turned down a $25 million offer while the company had $2 million in revenue (S004).
   - "you have to have a diversified mix of business that is something that was clear you cannot be exposed with all eggs in one basket" (S001@34:40)
   - "even if I think there is a big bubble going on there is a part of the technology that's real" (S004@51:27)
   - Sources: 3 (S001, S004, S008). Confidence: high.

## Building and product

5. **Build the product and earn quality before you sell abroad.** Export follows quality, not the other way round.
   - "Learn to build products. Then we'll get the export once we have a decent quality" (S007@39:36)
   - Sources: 2 (S007, S008). Confidence: medium.

6. **Copy first, then innovate.** Study the firms that already do it well; their methods are the starting point.
   - "If you don't know how to copy, you can never actually innovate" (S007@27:12)
   - Inferred from S001: a Swiss firm of about 20,000 people shows that if they can do it, you can too, so study such firms.
   - Sources: 2 (S001, S007). Confidence: medium.

7. **Build your own tools, IP and products rather than sell hours of service.** He regrets that the country went into services instead of products (S005).
   - "But what you should have built, we should have built is the intellectual property." (S005@25:24)
   - "If you don't make the tools yourselves, you won't be very good engineer in whatever we do." (S009@07:12)
   - Sources: 3 (S005, S008, S009). Confidence: high.

8. **Learn the domain by doing the work, not before it.** Domain knowledge comes from shipping.
   - "The domain expertise doesn't come before doing it." (S005@26:27)
   - "If you're a software engineer, become a domain expert" (S004@08:49)
   - Sources: 3 (S004, S005, S011). Confidence: high.

9. **Own your infrastructure at scale, and push the cost of AI down.** He says owning servers is cheaper than public cloud at scale, and that the answer to an expensive AI race is to be better, faster and cheaper, not bigger.
    - "it is cheaper to run your own servers and your own thing than the public cloud at scale" (S008@21:38)
    - "we can actually choose to be better, faster, cheaper, all of that." (S009@14:16)
    - Sources: 3 (S008, S009, S010). Confidence: high. S010 adds that the scale of AI investment cannot be justified by research use cases alone.

## AI

10. **A human takes responsibility for AI-assisted output.** Use AI for research and decision support, not for final output.
    - "As long as that thing is important, the human takes responsibility for the result" (S010@09:44)
    - "LLMs can give us an illusion of knowledge you start using them too early generate too much code you may never learn the skills." (S003@33:57)
    - Sources: 2 (S003, S010). Confidence: medium.
    - Dated views on AI productivity: in April 2025 he expected "five to 10fold productivity" through LLMs and compilers (S003@43:42, 2025-04-08), and he added "maybe in 6 months I may give you a different answer" (S003@42:36). In November 2025 he said "a 10x 100x productivity is possible" (S010@24:16, 2025-11-17, newer view). In the same talk he said he had not seen any dramatic gain yet (S010).

## Customers and trust

11. **Keep a human on customer support, and refund unhappy customers without argument.** Customers should talk to a person.
    - "we want that customer talking to a human being" (S005@16:47)
    - "if a customer is ever unhappy, we'll refund them the money, no questions asked." (S005@20:15)
    - Sources: 2 (S005, S010). Confidence: medium. S010 adds that AI should help the human agent, not replace them.

12. **Grow by word of mouth and cross-selling, not by heavy paid marketing.** He rejects the spend model of rivals that spend far more on sales and marketing (S001).
    - "We like to take care of customers and and word of mouth." (S005@17:26)
    - "we are not an advertising company and we are a very much a software company" (S011@04:04)
    - "we can now cross promote across our own product" (S008@18:48)
    - Sources: 4 (S001, S005, S008, S011). Confidence: high.

13. **Price for the value, and do not underprice.** Newer statements (2024 and 2025) say the price should reflect what the product is worth. The older statement from 2017 is the opposite and is kept as a tension.
    - Inferred from S001 (2024-04-12): he says he was told he was underpricing, and charges what the product is worth.
    - Inferred from S004 (2025-11-16): undercharging is a mistake, and the next customer should pay more.
    - Older view, S006 (2017-11-28): "keep the price very nominal very clean"
    - Sources: 2 newer (S001, S004); 1 older (S006). Confidence: medium. Newer view marked.

14. **Trust comes before commerce, and you accept lower profit to keep it.** Build the ecosystem on trust first.
    - "to build that ecosystem with confidence you need that foundation of trust" (S011@04:33)
    - "and of course the profit potential would be lower and that's okay" (S011@13:24)
    - Sources: 2 (S007, S011). Confidence: medium.

15. **Promise only what you can deliver, and state the limits honestly.** A missed promise costs more than a smaller one.
    - "if you promise to ship a million motors, we have to ship a million motors" (S007@40:16)
    - "we like to always tell the truth" (S010@13:35)
    - Sources: 3 (S007, S010, S011). Confidence: high. S011 adds that a company should not promise what it cannot guarantee.

## Where value and talent sit

16. **Keep the high-value share of trade and production at home.** Value moves to making, not trading.
    - "We cannot have a trade where we sell the low value added products" (S004@33:54)
    - "There is no semiconductor trading. It is all making." (S005@27:10)
    - Sources: 4 (S003, S004, S005, S011). Confidence: high.

17. **Put jobs where the talent is, and avoid metro concentration.** Jobs follow value added, and the roots of talent are in rural districts.
    - "You already have too many jobs. You have too many people. You have to disperse the jobs elsewhere." (S003@63:26)
    - "we don't want to make this a big city. We're going to spread this around" (S004@31:21)
    - Sources: 3 (S003, S004, S011). Confidence: high.

18. **Do not run a rural site from a distance; anchor it with a rooted local leader.** Put someone on the ground who will invest in people.
    - "One thing we don't accept is you can remotely manage this." (S012@12:22)
    - "somebody has to go and sit there and and invest in people" (S009@17:03)
    - Sources: 4 (S003, S004, S009, S012). Confidence: high.

19. **Hire for learnability, not credentials, and judge people by what they do.** Degrees and grades do not predict job performance. He does not question people's motives.
    - "the grades in college the degrees and the job performance don't correlate very well" (S008@24:26)
    - "I say this in every place in India there's talent like this. Everywhere." (S009@16:42)
    - "I don't try to question people's motivations" (S006@32:59)
    - Sources: 4 (S001, S006, S008, S009). Confidence: high. S001 says a smart 18-year-old can be trained for real work.

20. **Build talent before you need it, and do not assume ready-made talent exists.** Start the pipeline years before the site needs it.
    - "you cannot assume that readymade talent will be available in whatever area that you're working on" (S012@22:31)
    - "start at minus five years. Maybe take a five-year period as your talent creation" (S004@30:19)
    - Sources: 3 (S003, S004, S012). Confidence: high.

## Start and focus

21. **Start with humble, shippable problems, and test on a small group first.** Give a new team a small problem, test it internally, then build up.
    - "any new team I first ask them to solve some humble problems then build up build up build up" (S003@60:37)
    - "anywhere from a month to three months six months of internal trial before you actually launch in public" (S006@03:21)
    - Sources: 3 (S003, S006, S009). Confidence: high.

22. **Build shippable products that earn money, not papers.** Products are the goal; publishing is not.
    - "forget publishing papers in prestigious journals" (S003@59:31)
    - "we went in the direction of IT services and we should have gone into products more." (S005@23:35)
    - Sources: 2 (S003, S005). Confidence: medium.

23. **Pick a niche with few players, and name your difference before you enter a crowded market.** A small niche gives an easy start but a capped ceiling.
    - "if you find a niche which not a lot of players then easy to find customers" (S008@02:50)
    - "you have to find what is your differentiation what is currently lacking what is better about your solution" (S006@13:26)
    - Sources: 2 (S006, S008). Confidence: medium. S008 concedes that a small niche caps growth.

24. **Do not enter consumer (B2C) products without a strong foundation or a lot of capital, and plan for a ten-year horizon.** Koo had government backing but could not manage user-generated content, and it shut down (S012).
    - "you better have some strong either foundation from a a company like Zoho" (S012@24:56)
    - Sources: 1 (S012). Confidence: low. S008 gives a related caution that bootstrapping may not fit a hot market with a limited time span: "if you are in a currently hot market and if you have a limited time span" (S008@15:05).
