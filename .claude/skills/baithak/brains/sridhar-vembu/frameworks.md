# Frameworks: Sridhar Vembu

18 frameworks, most-used first. Built from 11 sources (S001, S003 to S012); S002 is excluded. "Sources" counts distinct sources in the corpus where the idea is used (ids listed). Names are his only where he gave one in the corpus; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. Lines marked "Guidance" are scoring guidance for the council built from his own numbers, not quotes. Lines marked "inferred from" combine several sources and are my reading.

## 1. Paid local training pipeline *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Pay young trainees a stipend straight out of school, train them in-house, then hire them full time after about a year and a half to two years. Trainees take on no debt.
- **Parts:** Recruit from nearby government schools at about 17 to 18; pay a stipend; train on real work; convert to full-time staff.
- **When to use:** Any plan that needs skilled people in a place where degrees are scarce or where the candidate pool is not yet proven.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if trainees are paid, carry no debt, and convert to hires within about two years; 3 if the program runs but conversion is unplanned; 1 if the plan depends on hiring graduates from outside the area.
- **Quote:** "so that they don't have to take on debt" (S008)
- **Limits:** The model was built over about 15 years (S008, Zoho University began with six students; about 150 a year by 2018). It is a long pipeline, not a quick hiring fix.
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S006, S008, S009)

## 2. Value-addition ladder and balance test *(unnamed; "rice paddy versus iPhone")*
- **What it is:** Compare what a district or company sells per kilogram with what it buys. Low value-added sales cannot pay for high value-added purchases, so prosperity needs a move up the value chain.
- **Parts:** List the outputs and their price per kilogram; list the inputs and their price per kilogram; check whether the sales side can pay for the purchases.
- **When to use:** Site choice, product choice, and any plan that sells raw or low-value output to buy finished goods.
- **How to score (guidance):** His figures: rice at 20 to 50 rupees per kilogram against an iPhone at about 3 lakh rupees per kilogram (S004), and rice at 25 rupees against smartphones at 1 lakh rupees per kilogram (S003). 5 if the sold output is clearly higher value than what is bought; 1 if the plan sells commodity output and buys finished goods.
- **Quote:** "If you sell rice patty at 25 rupees per kilogram and buy smartphones at uh 1 lakh rupees per kilogram, how can you ever balance the budget?" (S003)
- **Note:** S012 uses the same contrast as an analogy about switching suppliers: rice is easy to switch, an iPhone is not.
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S004, S012)

## 3. Anchor hire and rooted local leader *(inferred from S003, S004, S012)*
- **What it is:** A new site starts with one senior person who is rooted there and wants to commit. That person creates the culture, and the team grows around them. Open a centre only when committed local people are ready to run it.
- **Parts:** Find a rooted anchor; confirm they will commit for years; build the team around them; open only when the local team can run the site.
- **When to use:** Any new city or rural site.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if a rooted anchor is in place and has committed; 3 if the anchor is willing but has no local team yet; 1 if the site is planned from a head office.
- **Quote:** "You always need that anchor. And because that anchor is the one who creates the cultural glue for the rest of the company" (S004)
- **Limits:** He says one company cannot be everywhere (S003, "No one company can be everywhere."). The model needs a local leader in each place.
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S004, S012)

## 4. Domain expertise by building *(inferred from S004, S005, S011)*
- **What it is:** The domain knowledge comes from building and running the product, not from studying the domain first. Each product taught the company its domain as it was built.
- **Parts:** Build a first version in the domain; run it with real users; learn the domain from the failures and the customers.
- **When to use:** Entering a new vertical, or hiring for a team that must know a domain.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the team has already shipped in the domain; 3 if it has built adjacent products; 1 if the plan requires domain experts to be hired before any build.
- **Quote:** "Every single product we have built, we actually mastered the domain as we built the product" (S011)
- **Related quote:** "The domain expertise doesn't come before doing it." (S005)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S005, S011)

## 5. Start small and scale in stages *(inferred from S003, S006, S009)*
- **What it is:** A new team starts with humble, shippable problems, tests internally before public launch, and grows in steps. His rural example: five to 10 people at the start, about 1,000 people in 10 years, and about 5,000 in 15 to 20 years (S003).
- **Parts:** Give the team a small problem; test it inside the company for one to six months before public launch (S006); answer the query first and automate second (S009).
- **When to use:** Any new team, new product, or new site.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the first stage is small, shippable, and tested before scale; 1 if the plan starts at full scale.
- **Quote:** "any new team I first ask them to solve some humble problems then build up build up build up" (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S006, S009)

## 6. Study the competitor *(inferred from S001, S007, S010)*
- **What it is:** Learn from competitors and from the best products. Take the product apart, benchmark it against the best buyers, and read the competitor's regulatory filings, where they cannot exaggerate.
- **Parts:** Buy the competing product; take it apart; ask how a buyer in Dubai or Berlin would see it; read the filings.
- **When to use:** Before building a product that must compete on quality.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the plan names the rival product and the specific feature that wins; 1 if the plan has no benchmark.
- **Quote:** "Take apart a drone, a Chinese made drone. See how it's done" (S007)
- **Related quotes:** "how will Dubai customer see it how will Berlin customer see it" (S001); "You should study the competition's regulatory filings because that's one place they have to cut out the marketing" (S010)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S007, S010)

## 7. Ten-year staging and self-funded growth *(inferred from S003, S008, S012)*
- **What it is:** Fund the company from its own profits in stages. In his account, the first five years earn enough for what a first VC round would give, the next five years earn a later round, and by about ten years the company has public-company money with nothing raised. Reinvest profit in the next product (be your own VC).
- **Parts:** Commit to a long horizon; earn first; reinvest; avoid external capital that forces an exit.
- **When to use:** Any plan that must grow without outside money, or any site or segment with a long gestation.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if existing products fund the next product; 3 if a bridge is needed but planned; 1 if the plan needs a raise before first revenue.
- **Quote:** "the first five years is to earn enough capital the VC level of money" (S008)
- **Related quote:** "I'll spend 10 years if necessary that is vital" (S003)
- **Limits:** He concedes bootstrapping may not fit a hot market: "if you are in a currently hot market and if you have a limited time span" (S008). He also concedes that a small niche caps growth, and answers that profit can fund the next product (S008).
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S008, S012)

## 8. Teach by building *(inferred from S003, S009)*
- **What it is:** Students and new hires learn by building things, and theory comes after practice. He says a rural engineering college went from about 4 or 5 students a year to about 150 after students began building robots and drones (S003).
- **Parts:** Start with a build; learn the theory from what broke; avoid exam-only teaching.
- **When to use:** Hiring, training and any learning program.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if learners ship something in the first term; 1 if assessment is exams only.
- **Quote:** "don't inflict theory. Teach them practice." (S009)
- **Related quote:** "make sure you have a strong grounding in fundamentals by doing things not by writing exams" (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S009)

## 9. Catchment radius test *(inferred from S004, S012)*
- **What it is:** Do not ask whether talent exists in a place. Draw a 20 to 30 km radius and ask how long it will take to build the talent inside it. He says a random point in rural India gives about 3 to 5 lakh people inside that radius (S012). He starts talent building about five years before the need (S004).
- **Parts:** Draw the radius; count the population and the schools; estimate years to build the team; start the pipeline early.
- **When to use:** Choosing a rural or non-metro site.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the talent pool exists in the radius now; 3 if it must be built within about five years; 1 if the plan waits for talent that does not exist yet.
- **Quote:** "you drop a pin on the map in a random part of India, draw a 20 30 km radius" (S012)
- **Related quote:** "I don't ask is there going to be talent there? I ask how much time will it take us to create the talent" (S004)
- **Sources:** 2 (S004, S012)

## 10. Differentiation and niche test *(inferred from S006, S008)*
- **What it is:** Before you enter a crowded market, name the dimension where you are different: features, integration, pricing, or market access. Prefer a niche with few players, where customers are easy to find.
- **Parts:** List the dimensions of difference; pick one that is clear and lacking elsewhere; check how many players are in the niche.
- **When to use:** Entering a market with a large incumbent.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if one differentiator is clear and the niche has few players; 1 if the product is a copy in a crowded market.
- **Quote:** "you have to find what is your differentiation what is currently lacking what is better about your solution" (S006)
- **Related quote:** "if you find a niche which not a lot of players then easy to find customers" (S008)
- **Limits:** A small niche has a ceiling. He says growth in a small niche is capped, and that profit can be reinvested into the next product (S008).
- **Sources:** 2 (S006, S008)

## 11. Prototype-then-factory staging *(inferred from S003, S009)*
- **What it is:** Do the design and R&D first, with small prototyping in the local site. Iterate two or three times. Only then buy machinery and open a factory, and do production in an established hub until the factory stage.
- **Parts:** Build prototypes; test; iterate; study the manufacturing process, not only the machine; move to production.
- **When to use:** Any hardware or manufacturing plan.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if two or three prototype iterations passed before machinery is bought; 1 if machinery was bought before a working prototype.
- **Quote:** "Not just manufacturing, not just buy the machinery and open a factory, but also look into the process, manufacturing process" (S009)
- **Related quote:** "you do the design R&D in a rural area" (S003)
- **Limits:** He says approvals and friction rise at factory scale, and the rural R&D model does not solve that on its own (S003, inferred).
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S009)

## 12. Crore-scale cost check *(inferred from S001, S007)*
- **What it is:** Test a plan by its cost before you test its ambition. He gives crore-scale numbers: a metallurgy team at two to five crore a year, and a small motor factory at six to under ten crore.
- **Parts:** Estimate yearly cost; compare with his bands; check if the plan needs billions before revenue.
- **When to use:** Sizing R&D teams and first factories.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the plan fits his bands (two to five crore a year for a small R&D team; six to under ten crore for a small factory); 1 if the plan needs hundreds of crore before any revenue.
- **Quote:** "You can set up a motor factory, small motors, with about 6 to less than 10 crore investment" (S007)
- **Related quote:** "maybe two three crores a year five crores a year can actually get you a very good metallurgical team" (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S007)

## 13. Build the tool, then sell it *(inferred from S008, S009)*
- **What it is:** Build the tool you need for your own work, then turn it into a product. Make the tools that make the product, because a team that does not make its own tools will not be good at the work.
- **Parts:** Automate the internal process; use it yourself; then launch it as a product.
- **When to use:** Any software or hardware product where a team can use its own output first.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the product came out of a tool the team already uses daily; 1 if the team has never used what it sells.
- **Quote:** "we automated it ourselves and now we launched it as a product" (S008)
- **Related quote:** "If you don't make the tools yourselves, you won't be very good engineer in whatever we do." (S009)
- **Sources:** 2 (S008, S009)

## 14. Small models with a deterministic agent *(one source, S009)*
- **What it is:** Two parallel tracks. First, build small custom models for speech and OCR that run at the edge. Second, do energy-efficient R&D. In the product design, the language model handles the human input, and deterministic code does the actual work.
- **Parts:** Small specialist model at the edge; deterministic software for the work; the LLM only at the human interface.
- **When to use:** Designing an AI product where cost and reliability matter.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the LLM only reads and writes human language and deterministic code does the work; 1 if the LLM is doing the work itself.
- **Quote:** "Only the unstructured understanding the human is on the LLM level. All of the actual work is happening in the deterministic software." (S009)
- **Related quote:** "we have two parallel efforts going on. The first is of course producing the smaller models, smaller LLMs that are custom special purpose." (S009)
- **Sources:** 1 (S009)

## 15. AI critique loop *(one source, S010)*
- **What it is:** Ask one AI to critique its own answer in a new session, then set two or more AI tools against each other over five to ten alternatives. Use AI as a customised Wikipedia at a chosen depth, and verify the claims before you trust them.
- **Parts:** Generate; critique in a fresh session; compare with a second tool; verify claims.
- **When to use:** Research, query writing, and decision support. Not for final output.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the output is critiqued by a second tool and key claims are checked; 1 if the output is used unchecked.
- **Quote:** "Have two tabs open and set them against each other" (S010)
- **Limits:** Two AI tools once gave him different GPU information and left him confused. He says AI can mislead on a new topic (S010).
- **Sources:** 1 (S010)

## 16. Trust question *(one source, S011)*
- **What it is:** Any software use involves trust. Ask who you are trusting and whether you have legal recourse against them. Software security alone is not enough; institutions and law form a second layer.
- **Parts:** Name the party you trust; check the legal recourse; check the institutional layer.
- **When to use:** Any product that holds user data or payments.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if the trusted party is named and there is enforceable recourse; 1 if neither is clear.
- **Quote:** "The only remaining question is that who are you trusting and do you have a legal recourse to who you trust" (S011)
- **Sources:** 1 (S011)

## 17. Where-is-the-money-going test *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When a company says it needs money, ask where the money goes: into R&D with long-term relevance, or into buying customers for short-term growth.
- **Parts:** Ask for the spending split; identify R&D against customer acquisition; judge the answer.
- **When to use:** Any funding request, or any plan to raise money.
- **How to score (guidance):** 5 if most of the money goes to R&D or product; 1 if most of it buys customers.
- **Quote:** "where is the money going what are you spending that money on or are you spending it to build R&D or are you spending it just to acquire customers" (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001)

## 18. Spend-ratio red flag *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** A company that spends 70 to 80 percent on sales and marketing and about 10 percent on R&D is the pattern he rejects, even when it grows fast.
- **Parts:** Compare sales and marketing spend with R&D spend.
- **When to use:** Reviewing a company's spending plan or a competitor's.
- **How to score (guidance):** 1 if the plan is near 70 to 80 percent sales and marketing and about 10 percent R&D; 5 if R&D is the larger share.
- **Quote:** "they'll spend 70% sometimes 80% in sales and marketing they'll spend like 10% in R" (S001)
- **Sources:** 1 (S001)
