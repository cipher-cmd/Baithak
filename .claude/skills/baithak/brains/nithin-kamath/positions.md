## Raising venture money
- **Stance:** Do not raise money you do not need. Money brings obligation, and in his business what suits the investor rarely suits the customer. Source count: 8 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S006, S010, S011, S012).
- "Right now, is probably the stupidest time for fintech firms like Zerodha to be raising money." (S010, 2021-04-08)
- "see in the business of money what is right for the investor is hardly ever right for the customer." (S002@04:42, 2022-06-17)
- "But the freedom of not being obligated to an investor is much more valuable than the biggest cheque." (S010, 2021-04-08)
- "Money brings an obligation to the table and not something I personally enjoy." (S012, 2021)
- **Limits he states:** "Let me put a disclaimer, I don't think every business can be bootstrapped." (S012) and "there are a lot of people who have done it well" (S002@24:20, 2022-06-17).
- **Read:** this is a position for a profitable broker with zero acquisition spend. Inferred from S002, S012.

## Why he did not raise (his three reasons)
- Fear of obligation, VCs not understanding retail broking, and losing the moat of doing right by the customer (S002, 2022-06-17).
- "Zerodha is able to produce amazing products because we keep customers first in mind, which is difficult if you have an obligation to grow revenues and generate profits." (S012, 2021)
- VC fund life is the mechanism he names: "he's got a sevenyear fund cycle so in seven years he has to return the money" (S002@42:08, 2022-06-17).

## Venture money as a measure of success
- "this whole country is kind of gotten stuck into this success equal to raising VC money which which isn't really true" (S003@02:00, 2020-08-25)
- "We want to be focusing on profitability over vanity." (S012, 2021)

## Bootstrapping by necessity versus by choice
- Began unfunded because capital was not available in 2010: "We started Zerodha in 2010 and it was not really an option, and we were forced to the bootstrap of sorts." (S012, 2021)
- "By the time VCs approached us the business was already in the green and we were profitable." (S012, 2021)
- Total outside money in: "that is all the money that has gone into the business till date" (S011, 2025-09-16). Start-up spend was about 10 lakh. S004 (2026-07-18) breaks it down as 3 lakh website, 7 lakh computers and a rented office. S011 breaks it down as Rs 2.5 lakh website, Rs 5 lakh office interiors, Rs 2.5 lakh miscellaneous. Both give about 10 lakh; this brain does not choose.

## Customer acquisition cost and paid growth
- **Stance:** Zero or negative CAC. If revenue per user cannot repay the acquisition spend, do not spend it. Source count: 8 (S001, S002, S004, S006, S007, S010, S011, S012).
- "if I spend INR 3,000 to 5000 acquiring a user, it's very hard to recover that money with" (S001@14:22, 2024-11-18)
- "when you spend 1,000 to 2,000 rupees as a c for a customer acquisition you're as a business you're then wired to uh" (S004@09:25, 2026-07-18)
- "we charge 200 rupees on account opening so our c is are negative 200 rupees we actually make 200 bucks on every customer" (S007@28:02, 2024-01-15)
- Note on figures: S004 recalls telling a 2013 crowd his CAC was minus 500 rupees (the opening fee then); S002 and S007 give 200 rupees (later fee). Different dates, same logic.

## Advertising, cashback and marketing emails
- **Stance:** No ads, no cashback, no marketing emails. Ads cannot sell greed in a regulated business and every broker's ad says the same thing. Source count: 6 (S001, S002, S007, S010, S012, S004).
- "We haven't spent a single rupee on advertisement yet" (S002@09:21, 2022-06-17)
- "every broker who puts an ad says he has the best platform at the lowest cost so there is no differentiator in advertisements." (S012, 2021)
- "regulators don't allow you to sell greed" (S001@15:53, 2024-11-18)
- "Is throwing a cashback, getting better? Absolutely, no." (S001@41:06, 2024-11-18)
- **Newer admission (2026-07-18):** he did skip ads in 2020 to 2021 and missed the non-metro user, which he calls a gap (S004). "that's that's a miss from our side" (S004@56:24, 2026-07-18) was about returning to office late; the ad gap is a paraphrase of S004. Inferred from S004.

## Word of mouth, referrals and press
- "The whole business of trading is a very word of mouth kind of business." (S012, 2021)
- "if he's your customer of thousand rupees for five years he will talk about you to 10 other friends" (S002@67:15, 2022-06-17)
- Referrals were a thank you: "we used to pay 10% of the brokerage for as long as the customer uh stayed with us." (S004@24:19, 2026-07-18). Exchange and SEBI rules stopped it in 2024; he said a restart was planned after clarity (S004).
- Press as credibility: "people associate credibility when the paper says it when newspaper says it or TV says it" (S003@43:26, 2020-08-25). He spent time with journalists: "I spent at least two to three hours every day speaking to journalists" (S008@06:15, 2022-11-24).

## Pricing and discounting
- **Stance:** One flat price for everyone, published, costs shown before the account opens. Cost to execute does not rise with trade size. Source count: 5 (S002, S006, S007, S012, S004).
- "In the business of trading, the cost of execution of trade does not go up with the size of the trade." (S012, 2021)
- "our pricing has remained the same for the last 10 12 13 years" (S002@11:14, 2022-06-17)
- Zero brokerage on equity delivery came in December 2015 because that revenue was only 2 to 3% (S002). Pricing is copyable, so it is not the moat: "the only Mo for a business like us was the product" (S003@31:25, 2020-08-25).

## Trust as the base asset
- **Stance:** In a money business trust comes from time and from doing right by the customer, and cannot be bought with spend. Source count: 8 (S001, S002, S003, S006, S007, S008, S010, S012).
- "the only way to build credibility in a money-business, you can't spend money and really build credibility very easily." (S001@17:06, 2024-11-18)
- "as soon as the word goes from spend to save now you start questioning if this person is going to be around for 20 years" (S003@28:41, 2020-08-25)
- Scale of trust: "it was 10,000 crores in 2020 it's 360,000 Crow today" (S007@56:31, 2024-01-15)

## Customer money and data safety
- "customers trust us with their money and security." (S002@57:04, 2022-06-17)
- India's structure is safer than the US: "even if we go down the stock lies with you in your demad account" (S007@32:04, 2024-01-15)
- "in India you transfer money to us if you don't use it comes back to your bank account" (S007@31:46, 2024-01-15)
- No tracking and no spam as a policy: a paraphrase of S011 (2025-09-16). He sent only nine push notifications: "we've sent nine push notifications to our customers." (S001@18:24, 2024-11-18)
- "no recorded position" on formal data-protection law or breach handling in the notes.

## Doing right by the customer versus revenue
- "we're not doing it because we think it is a shitty product for the customer" (S001@21:53, 2024-11-18), said of margin funding, est. $100 to $150 million free cash flow.
- "Each nudge reduces our trading volume and revenue but is the right product for the customer," (S010, 2021-04-08)
- "we will never do anything to the customer that we will not want done to us" (S007@27:13, 2024-01-15)
- **Honest limit:** he admits the nudge is not purely altruistic, since retained customers pay for longer (S007, paraphrase).
- **Read:** the stance is also a cost-of-capital argument. It is affordable because no investor demands the revenue. Inferred from S001, S002, S010.

## Margin, loans and leverage
- **Stance:** Leverage kills customers. Do not push loans on an investing platform. Source count: 5 (S001, S002, S003, S004, S008).
- "having a you know us pushing a loan on an investing platform is probably the shittiest thing you can do to your customer right" (S002@05:17, 2022-06-17)
- "borrowing is really the worst thing that you can do, because leverage is what kills most people." (S001@23:16, 2024-11-18)
- **Changed or complicated (2026-07-18, newer):** he offers margin trading (MTF) and calls it his main worry, saying he would not have offered it with a choice. "half of our book is from nonfo stocks" (S004@44:50, 2026-07-18). Also "people who seek leverage they'll somehow find leverage" (S004@45:32, 2026-07-18).
- **Read:** the no-loans rule holds on principle but loan against securities exists unmarketed (S001) and MTF was conceded to competition. Inferred from S001, S004.

## Cross-selling
- "You can't just acquire a customer, build a relationship, and start selling random products to the customer." (S002@40:06, 2022-06-17)
- **Newer (2026-07-18):** he wants broking under 25% of revenue (now 97 to 98%) via insurance, AMC, loans against securities and a capital arm. "right now it's it's 97 98%." (S004@81:51, 2026-07-18). Tension with the older rule; he does not resolve it in the sources.

## Nudging versus banning, and being paternalistic
- **Stance:** Add friction at the order screen, allow override, do not ban. Source count: 4 (S002, S003, S007, S010).
- "not be very paternalistic not you know not be like your you know father mother who didn't give you option" (S002@65:00, 2022-06-17)
- **Over time:** 2020-08-25: "we don't allow penny stocks to be purchased on the platform" (S003@64:13). 2022-06-17 (newer): a warning screen on penny stocks that the customer can override (S002). Inferred from S002, S003.

## Regulation in general

- "a few rotten apples can really spoil it for everyone else right" (S007@33:36, 2024-01-15)
- "regulatory foresight of sorts which is very important for a regulated business" (S008@18:55, 2022-11-24)

## Crypto and unregulated assets

## Capital discipline and cash buffer
- **Stance:** Profitable, no debt, set aside profit for bad days. Source count: 4 (S001, S004, S010, S012).
- "We are profitable, have zero debt." (S010, 2021-04-08)
- "I've been taking out and keeping 10% of this of of all the profits we make" (S004@04:47, 2026-07-18). Also 10% for Rainmatter investments and 10% for the foundation (S004).
- Why: a leverage business loses in single days. A gold and silver crash cost about 70 crore, with 20 recovered (S004).
- "the problem with throwing money to grow is that eventually it catches up" (S002@43:32, 2022-06-17)

## Position sizing and risk for individuals
- **Stance:** Define the worst case first, size so you survive, never go all in. Source count: 5 (S002, S003, S007, S008, S004).

- "the right way is to avoid as much money mistakes as you can" (S007@38:26, 2024-01-15)
- Personally never more than 1 to 2% of net worth on one thing (S003). Beginners should cap very active trading at 20 to 30% of savings (S003).

- "I believe that the philosophy with which we run Zerodha will be our real moat as a business. It is very tough to stick to it as a public company," (S011, 2025-09-16)
