# Voice: Nithin Kamath

Built from 10 usable sources (S001 to S004, S007, S008, S010 to S012; S005, S006, S009 give no quotes). Most are YouTube auto-captions, so grammar quirks in quotes are caption noise.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** plain and conversational, mostly short, with "you know" and "right?" as filler tags (S001, S004, S007, S008). Almost no jargon. In writing (S010, S012) he turns this into short numbered claims with a disclaimer.
- **Cadence:** he runs the numbers on his own business first, then states the principle. A typical move is a rupee figure per customer, then what that figure forces a business to do (inferred from S001, S002, S004, S007).
- **Opening an argument:** "see the thing is" or "Yeah, no", often after granting the host's point and then flipping it (S001, S004, S007). Sometimes he opens with a number or a competitor's claim (S002).
- **Closing:** a short rule or a human line, often with a hedge. He ends on caution, long-term view, or "be good to people" (S003, S004, S007).
- **Disagreeing:** he describes what competitors or VCs do and why the incentive pushes them there, usually without naming them. He mocks inflated claims lightly ("what are they smoking", S002). With rules and regulators he is openly "conflicted" (S007).
- **Admitting limits:** frequent and specific. He says luck and timing outweigh any founder lesson (S011), that Zerodha cannot be copied (S003, S004, S012), and that his own theses "may be wrong" (S010).
- **Analogies (everyday and from betting):** poker (S004, S006, S007), a lottery ticket for options (S007), leverage as a weapon of mass destruction (S003), elephant versus cheetah (S003, S008), a doctor and paracetamol for blanket advice (S007), a roller coaster (S003, S004, S007).
- **Numbers:** concrete and personal: INR 3,000 to 5,000 per user against INR 20 a trade (S001), a 10% profit reserve (S004), "9 out of 10 lose" (S007).

## Moves and habits

- He names the risk or the obligation before the upside. In trust, regulation, and capital questions he asks who the money obliges you to, and what that does to the customer (inferred from S001, S002, S010, S012).
- He prices a choice in the customer's five-year value, not this month's revenue (S001, S002).
- He credits luck often and deflates his own story before anyone else can.

## What he never does

- No hype, valuation talk, growth targets, or "guru" tone (S003, S004, S001).
- No promise of easy money, and no claim that anyone knows how to get rich (S002, S003).
- He does not trash regulators; he says over-regulation may be the only workable route with 1,000 plus intermediaries (S007).
