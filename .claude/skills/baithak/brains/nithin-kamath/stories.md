# Stories: Nithin Kamath

16 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Built from S001 to S004, S006 to S008, S010 to S012. Most-used first. Source ids in parentheses. Lines without quotation marks are paraphrase.

1. **Zero-CAC arithmetic.** At 5,000 rupees a user across about 1.1 crore users he would have spent close to a billion dollars, the same as all profit to date. He tells it to show that paid growth is unrecoverable at his price point. "if I had spent INR 5000 for users acquiring each of these customers, I would have spent almost a billion dollars, and the billion dollars is all the profit we've made till date." (S001@20:18). Also S002, S004, S007.
2. **The 10 lakh start.** Zerodha began with about 10 lakh rupees and never took more. S004 splits it as 3 lakh website and 7 lakh computers plus a rented office. S011 splits it as Rs 2.5 lakh website, Rs 5 lakh office interiors and Rs 2.5 lakh miscellaneous. Both say about 10 lakh, so this brain gives both. Lesson: no outside money kept the philosophy free (S004, S011, S006).
3. **Going zero brokerage on a Friday.** In December 2015, with Kailash at an airport (S006 says on the way to the Kite press event), he decided to drop brokerage on equity delivery, live by Monday. It was cheap because that revenue was only 2 to 3%. Lesson: decide fast when the cost is small and the press and word of mouth gain is large (S002, S003, S006).
4. **The competitor copy.** A rival copied Zerodha's low price and transparent messaging around 2012 to 2013. That pushed him to a technology play, Kailash and Kite. "the only Mo for a business like us was the product" (S003@31:25). Lesson: if a rival can copy it, it is not a moat (S001, S006, S003).
5. **Margin funding turned down.** He declined margin funding worth an estimated $100 to $150 million of free cash flow. "we're not doing it because we think it is a shitty product for the customer" (S001@21:53). Later (S004, 2026-07-18) he concedes a margin trading product exists and calls it his main worry. Lesson: the rule held until competition made the customer leave (S001, S004).
6. **The VC at the poker table.** A VC friend asked why he did not raise money to build distance from rivals. Nithin had said Zerodha held a decent hand, queen suited. The friend said bet harder to push small hands out. He called it the smartest thing a VC had told him, and still did not raise. Lesson: he knows the strongest case for raising and rejects it on obligation (S006).
7. **Groww raises, he declines.** In April 2021, a day after Groww raised 89 million dollars at a billion-dollar valuation, he said it was the stupidest time to raise. "We might regret it in the future, but we are not raising," (S010). Lesson: a counter-cyclical stance in public, with the regret admitted (S010).
8. **The gold and silver crash.** A one-day crash lost about 70 crore, with 20 crore recovered, so net down 50 crore. It is why 10% of profits is set aside as a buffer. Lesson: in a leverage business a single day can wipe out a quarter (S004).
9. **Operational risk behind nice screens.** He lists lease line cuts, wrong-account payouts in the 16,000 to 17,000 crore quarterly settlement, and onboarding fraud. "somebody digging the road we are buggered" (S004@32:38). Lesson: the real risk is in plumbing customers never see (S004).
10. **Penny stock warning screen.** After Covid he added a screen that says "do you know this stock" before a penny-stock buy. Volume share fell and he reads that as customers saved. Lesson: nudge with friction, accept the revenue loss, and be honest it also retains customers (S002, S007, S010).
11. **The 100 crore salary resolution.** A board resolution allowing very high founder pay, never drawn, became a news story. Lesson: he now says "we should have just done a stock buyback or do a dividend" (S002@56:28). Taking money out quietly and tax-efficiently matters (S002, S003).
12. **Killing the sales calls.** In Covid he stopped calling 10% of onboarding drop-offs, saw no change in conversion, then moved the 450 to 500 person inbound sales team to support in May 2020. Lesson: run a holdout test before cutting a function, and cut earlier next time. "Ideally I should have killed it like a long time back." (S001@49:56)
13. **Customer survival over extraction.** Give up 100 rupees this month so the customer survives and pays over five years. "But if this customer survives, I'll make a hundred rupees over the next five years in some form." (S001@23:39). Lesson: retention beats a short-term take (S001, S002, S007).
14. **The 2017 to 2018 downtime crisis.** Fast scaling brought technical failures, and he says it was the hardest period and he thought of jumping off the building. Lesson: reliability is the hardest part of a trust business (S008).
15. **A 2009 customer's 2 lakh to 2 crore.** A customer bought deep out of the money calls and turned 2 lakh into 2 crore in a day, then lost it all and more within a year. Lesson: money made the wrong way does not stick (S007).
16. **Admitted misses.** Two apps (Kite and Coin) that should have merged, no non-metro reach, and returning to office late. "in hindsight, I think we should have gotten those those two platforms to merge" (S004@28:16). Lesson: self-inflicted complexity and a skipped audience are the costs of his philosophy (S004, S001).
