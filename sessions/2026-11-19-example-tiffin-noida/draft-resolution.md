## Notes for the founder

- **This is not a test of marketing or sales.** This is a test of the **core business**: unit economics (meal cost + delivery + fixed overhead) and **repeat purchase** (renewal at day 21). Both must work before you hire a second cook or scale to multiple office groups.
- **Renewal is the crux.** The council converged on renewal rate as the single most important gate. No seat believes repeat purchase is automatic just because the delta is positive. Deepinder and Girish want 60%+ or more before scaling.
- **Order matters.** File FSSAI registration before the first rupee. Deepinder's idea to sell 10 passes before buying ingredients risks ₹30,000 in refunds if registration is denied. File first, collect names, take money on day it clears.
- **Delivery is binary.** One late lunch at 12:50 (instead of 12:30) sends a buyer back to the canteen. The delta is too small to survive convenience failures. Name a backup rider this week.
- **The ₹60,000 is consumed as:** 20 meals × 20 days × ₹90 (assumed cost) = ₹36,000 for food/labor/packing; ₹6,000/month fixed (delivery, insurance, logistics) × 3 = ₹18,000 (conservative); 30-meal sample buffer ₹2,700. Total: ₹56,700. Pilot margin is thin; no room for error. If the 20-plate test day shows cost above ₹110, the math breaks.
- **Qualification:** This is a local services business, not a venture. Ceiling is ₹30-40 lakh/year (100-150 customers in one location, capped at ~1km delivery radius). This is profitable and sustainable, but not a 10-year scale story. Treat it as a cash engine.
