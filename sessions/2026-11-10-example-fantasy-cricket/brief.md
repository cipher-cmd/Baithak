# Brief: fantasy cricket for Tier-2 college students

Mode: --quick (four seats, Rounds 1 and 2; Round 3 only if scores do not converge). Founder said "just run it"; gaps are filled with assumptions marked (assumed).

## The decision
1. **The idea:** a fantasy cricket app where users pay Rs 49 to enter contests and win cash prizes, aimed at Tier-2 college students.
   - **A (as pitched):** paid-entry fantasy cricket, Rs 49 per contest, cash prizes.
   - **B (Chair's reframe):** free-to-play fantasy cricket for the same students. No entry fee, no cash or cash-convertible prizes. Revenue from ads, brand-sponsored college leagues, and an optional paid pass that removes ads (the pass wins nothing).
   - **C:** don't build it.
2. **Who pays:** A: India 2 students pay entry fees. B: advertisers and sponsors pay; a few students pay for the ad-free pass (assumed).
2a. **Idea type:** 2. Consumer app and content, with secondary type 8 (money staked in A).
3. **Today's workaround:** students follow cricket on free apps and TV, play free fantasy or prediction games among friends, and talk in WhatsApp groups (assumed).
4. **Money flow and first 20 customers:** A: UPI per contest, platform keeps a rake (assumed 15 to 20%). B: sponsor deals per college league (assumed). First 20 users through college WhatsApp groups and campus ambassadors (assumed).
5. **Resources:** Rs 10 lakh. Founder full time (assumed). No deadline given; the cricket calendar sets one (assumed).
6. **Success:** paying or retained users by the next major cricket season (assumed).
7. **Founder's lean:** yes, "because Dream11 proved the market".
8. **Evidence:** level 0, founder intuition. The market Dream11 proved was paid-entry fantasy, the model the brief now asks about.
