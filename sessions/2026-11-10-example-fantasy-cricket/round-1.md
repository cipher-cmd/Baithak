# Round 1

## Nithin

SEAT: Nithin (Trust, regulation, and capital discipline)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on B, and only as a Rs 2 lakh test)
OPTION SCORES: A 1/10 · B 5/10 · C 4/10
PILLAR SCORE: 5/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none (B), likely (A)
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Build B as a cheap free-to-play league test, cap spend at Rs 2 lakh of the Rs 10 lakh, and kill A outright.

WHY:
1. Yeah, no, Dream11 proved the market, but it proved paid entry, which is exactly what the Chair marks BLACK. A is unusable. Also, a Rs 49 entry with an assumed 15 to 20% rake earns Rs 7 to 10 per entry, and your revenue only rises when students keep losing. That is wired against trust. The signature rule applies: "we will never do anything to the customer that we will not want done to us" (S007). Taking money from students to run a contest the law bars fails that test and every other.
2. B needs unit economics, and they are thin. I'm assuming Rs 5 to 10 a month in ad and sponsor revenue per active student. That means 10,000 monthly actives bring in about Rs 50,000 to 1 lakh a month, before servers and match data. Nobody has shown that sponsors will pay. Zero CAC (S001, S012) only works if campus WhatsApp groups bring users free. That is untested and level 0.
3. Capital discipline: the Rs 10 lakh is your freedom, so don't spend it all on an unproven habit. "But the freedom of not being obligated to an investor is much more valuable than the biggest cheque." (S010) Don't raise on seasonal cricket and low ad yield either. The Chair marks scalability CAPPED, and I agree.

THE RISK I SEE: Trust with minors. A student app collects age and phone data, and one cash-convertible prize, or one gift voucher that works like cash, pulls B back toward A. Also Tier-2 students may have no reason to leave free apps they already use.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Two signed sponsors, or 500 students who come back in a non-match week without any prize. That moves B to 7. If neither shows up by one tournament, I move to C. I'd move on A only if a lawyer says in writing that it is legal, and I don't expect that.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Get a lawyer's written opinion on B, covering the age gate and the prize rules. Run one 3-college league on WhatsApp and a spreadsheet with no app build. Ask two local brands for Rs 25,000 each.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: If no student pays and no prize is offered, why would they open your app on a day without a match?

## Kunal

SEAT: Kunal (Consumer behaviour and India 1 vs Bharat)
STANCE: Yes (on C, with the test variant below)

THE CALL: Don't build free-to-play B as pitched; run a two-week uncoded test of one variant, college-vs-college leagues that students can brag about, and build only if students say yes.

WHY:
1. Delta 4, jugaad test (S001, S010). Today's workaround is free fantasy on big apps plus WhatsApp prediction games. I'd score it 6/10 (assumed). B copies it, a 7 at best. Gap of 1. The Chair grades Delta "AT RISK"; I say it fails. "share in Delta 1 category it just erods value" (S010). The Rs 10 lakh would be spent on a reversible switch.
2. Rivers and dams (S007, S002). Dream11 proved a river: money on the line plus status. B removes the money, so the river is gone and only the dam is left. "Most tech founders are great at building dams and terrible at finding rivers." (S007) The founder's "Dream11 proved it" is evidence for A, which is BLACK, not for B.
3. Who pays (S001, S007). The payer in B is an advertiser. "India becomes a great MAU farm but very low on ARPU." (S001) Tier-2 students are the lowest-ARPU slice, and the paid ad-free pass has to beat free. "Will they pay 1 rupee?" (S007) Nobody has asked them. Assumed: under 3% buy a pass at Rs 49. That is 1,000 payers per 35,000 users, about Rs 49,000.
Where I side with the Chair: sponsors are the only real money. Status is the one thing a free version can offer, so test it first.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Blunt students, not friends, scoring the league at 4 or more points over their WhatsApp game. Or two brands saying in writing they would sponsor a league. Either moves B to a 6.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Make a slide of a "my college vs your college" league and show it to 30 students in 3 colleges, plus 2 brand managers. Build nothing. Count how many ask when it launches. Target: 95% excited, as in S010.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: Name one student who would stop using their current free game for yours, and say what you would tell them to make them switch. If you can't, who is B for?

## Deepinder

SEAT: Deepinder (Consumer habit and operations at scale)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on B)
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10 · B 5/10 · C 4/10
PILLAR SCORE: 4/10 (Frequency and operational scale, judged on B alone)
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: possible
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Build B as a single-college free league, capped at Rs 2.5 lakh a month in spend, and kill it if the habit numbers miss by the end of the first tournament.

WHY:
1. A has the habit and it is banned. Fantasy is a repeat product, and I'd normally score it high. But the Chair marks A BLACK, so I score it 2. B keeps the repeat but loses the thing that made Dream11 stick, the money. "Consumer behaviour is the hardest thing in the world to change" (S013). Free games already exist, so B has to beat them.
2. Peak load is the toss. This is the habit versus general-transaction test (S013), and I'd rank match day against dinner rush. My inference: most team entries land in the 15 minutes before lock. I assume 20,000 daily users with 60% of entries in that window, so about 12,000 entries in 15 minutes. That is cheap for a free app on static scoring, but a crash at the toss is a brand death. "so you have to let go of those orders in order to maintain some balance on cost" (S008). I'd queue or cap entries before I'd crash.
3. Walk one user in rupees. I assume ad yield of Rs 1 per daily user per match day and 60 match days a year. That is Rs 60 a user a year, or about Rs 12 lakh on 20,000 users. An ad-free pass at Rs 99 and 2% take-up adds roughly Rs 40,000. That doesn't cover a team. Cash discipline: "we always kept four months' money in the bank" (S004). Rs 10 lakh over four months is Rs 2.5 lakh a month.

THE RISK I SEE: Cricket is seasonal, so the Chair's retention check is worse than AT RISK. Usage collapses between tournaments and sponsors pay late. This is a low-frequency product for about ten months a year, and my habit test can wrongly kill it. I say so.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Upward, if one college hits 40% weekly return in the first two weeks of a tournament. Downward, if paid sponsor interest is zero after 10 pitches.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Pick one college and one live match. Run a manual WhatsApp league and count who returns for match two. Write the toss-time load estimate on paper.

QUESTION FOR THE FOUNDER: What will a student do on this app on a day with no match?
